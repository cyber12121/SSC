## ⚡ Rule 1: Always Plural Nouns (Take Plural Verb)
**Formula / Structure:** `Noun (Always Plural Form) + Plural Verb`

These nouns are always used in the plural form only and cannot be made singular by removing 's'/'es'. They always take a plural verb.

- **Instruments / Tools:** Scissors, Tongs, Pliers, Pincers, Bellows
- **Clothing / Accessories:** Trousers, Pants, Pajamas, Shorts, Spectacles, Glasses, Goggles, Binoculars, Sunglasses
- **Other Common SSC Nouns:** Alms, Amends, Archives, Arrears, Auspices, Congratulations, Embers, Fireworks, Lodgings, Outskirts, Particulars, Proceeds, Regards, Remains, Savings, Shambles, Surroundings, Tidings, Troops, Tactics, Thanks, Valuables, Wages, Belongings

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *Where is my scissors?*  
  ✔️ **Where are my scissors?**
- ❌ *All his belongings was lost.*  
  ✔️ **All his belongings were lost.**
- ❌ *I bought a spectacles.*  
  ✔️ **I bought a pair of spectacles.**

> 💡 **Key SSC Note:** To indicate singular count, use 'a pair of...'. When 'a pair of' is used as the subject, the verb becomes singular ('A pair of trousers was bought').

## ⚡ Rule 2: Plural in Form, but Singular in Meaning (Take Singular Verb)
**Formula / Structure:** `Plural-looking Noun + Singular Verb`

These nouns end in 's' and look plural, but their meaning is singular and they always take a singular verb.

- **Subjects / Branches of Study:** Mathematics, Physics, Ethics, Economics, Classics, Politics, Statistics, Linguistics
- **Diseases:** Measles, Mumps, Rickets, Shingles
- **Games / Sports:** Billiards, Darts, Draughts, Athletics
- **General:** News, Innings, Summons, Gallows

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *Mathematics are a difficult subject.*  
  ✔️ **Mathematics is a difficult subject.**
- ❌ *No news are good news.*  
  ✔️ **No news is good news.**
- ❌ *He was issued a summon.*  
  ✔️ **He was issued a summons (Singular = summons; Plural = summonses).**

> ⚠️ **Special Exception:** When subjects like Mathematics, Politics, Statistics are preceded by a possessive adjective (my, his, our) or 'the' to mean calculations, political views, or data, they take a PLURAL verb: 'His mathematics are very weak', 'The statistics of the survey reveal...'

## ⚡ Rule 3: Singular in Form, but Always Plural in Meaning (Take Plural Verb)
**Formula / Structure:** `Noun (No -s ending) + Plural Verb`

These nouns look singular because they lack an 's' ending, but they are plural in sense and always take a plural verb. Never add '-s' or '-es' to them.

- **Complete Word List:** Cattle, Cavalry, Infantry, Poultry, Peasantry, Children, Gentry, Police, People, Folk, Company

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *Cattles are grazing in the field.*  
  ✔️ **Cattle are grazing in the field (Never say 'cattles').**
- ❌ *Police has caught the thief.*  
  ✔️ **Police have caught the thief.**
- ❌ *Our infantry has marched forward.*  
  ✔️ **Our infantry have marched forward.**

> ⚠️ **Special Exception:** 'People' means human beings. 'Peoples' is used ONLY when referring to nations/ethnic groups of the world.

## ⚡ Rule 4: Uncountable Nouns (Always Singular Verb, No a/an, No -s/-es)
**Formula / Structure:** `Uncountable Noun + Singular Verb [Never take a/an, never take -s/-es]`

These nouns are strictly uncountable. They cannot be pluralized by adding 's' and never take indefinite articles (a/an) directly.

- **Complete Word List:** Scenery, Poetry, Furniture, Advice, Information, Luggage, Baggage, Postage, Knowledge, Jewelry, Work, Equipment, Bread, Cutlery, Confectionery, Crockery, Alphabet, Percentage, Dirt, Dust, Traffic, Electricity, Money, Music, Garbage, Cost, Fuel, Paper

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *The sceneries of Kashmir are beautiful.*  
  ✔️ **The scenery of Kashmir is beautiful.**
- ❌ *He gave me an advice.*  
  ✔️ **He gave me a piece of advice.**
- ❌ *I have many works to do.*  
  ✔️ **I have much work to do (or: many pieces of work).**
- ❌ *Furnitures were sold.*  
  ✔️ **Furniture was sold.**

## ⚡ Rule 5: Usage of 'Hair' (Uncountable vs Countable)
**Formula / Structure:** `All hair on head = Uncountable (Singular) | Individual strands = Countable (Plural)`

Hair is normally considered an uncountable collective noun taking a singular verb. However, when counting specific individual strands, it can be pluralized.

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *His hairs are black.*  
  ✔️ **His hair is black.**
- ❌ *She found two hair in the soup.*  
  ✔️ **She found two hairs in the soup (Countable strands).**

## ⚡ Rule 6: Nouns with Identical Singular & Plural Forms
**Formula / Structure:** `Same Spelling for 1 or Many (Verb depends on context)`

These nouns do not change form in the plural. Their verb is determined by whether the context refers to one or many.

- **Complete Word List:** Deer, Sheep, Fish, Offspring, Aircraft, Spacecraft, Series, Species, Jury, Team, Crew, Family, Council

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *A sheep is grazing / Sheeps are grazing.*  
  ✔️ **A sheep is grazing / Sheep are grazing (Never 'sheeps').**
- ❌ *Many deers were seen.*  
  ✔️ **Many deer were seen (Never 'deers').**
- ❌ *A new series are launched.*  
  ✔️ **A new series is launched / Two new series are launched.**

> ⚠️ **Special Exception:** 'Fishes' is only used when referring to different species or varieties of fish, not total quantity.

## ⚡ Rule 7: [The + Common Noun] Expressing an Abstract Quality
**Formula / Structure:** `'The' + Common Noun = Abstract Quality / Feeling`

When 'the' is prefixed to a common noun representing a person or relation, it denotes the inner emotion, instinct, or characteristic (functioning as an abstract noun).

- **Example:** *The judge in him prevailed over the father.* ('The judge' = sense of justice; 'the father' = fatherly affection.)
- **Example:** *The mother in her arose when she saw the crying orphan.* ('The mother' = maternal instinct.)

---

## ⚡ Rule 8: Plural of Nouns Ending in '-ful'
**Formula / Structure:** `Base + ful + 's' -> (cupfuls, handfuls)`

Nouns ending in 'ful' are pluralized by adding 's' at the very end of 'ful', NOT to the base noun.

- **Complete Word List:** Cupfuls, Handfuls, Glassfuls, Spoonfuls, Mouthfuls, Bucketfuls

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *He drank three cupsful of coffee.*  
  ✔️ **He drank three cupfuls of coffee.**
- ❌ *Take two spoonsful of syrup.*  
  ✔️ **Take two spoonfuls of syrup.**

---

## ⚡ Rule 9: Precise Usage of 'Half'
**Formula / Structure:** `Half an hour / A half-hour | Two and a half hours / Two hours and a half`

Specific structural conventions govern how 'half' pairs with time and units:

- • 'Half an hour' OR 'A half-hour' (NOT 'A half an hour')
- • 'A half mile' OR 'Half a mile'
- • 'One and a half hours' (Plural unit)
- • 'Two and a half hours' OR 'Two hours and a half'

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *I waited for a half an hour.*  
  ✔️ **I waited for half an hour (or a half-hour).**

---

## ⚡ Rule 10: Hyphenated Compound Nouns Used as Adjectives
**Formula / Structure:** `Numeral + Hyphen + Singular Noun (Never Pluralized)`

When a compound noun with a numeral functions as an adjective before another noun, the hyphenated noun must ALWAYS be singular.


> 💡 **Key SSC Note:** If not modifying another noun, the noun is plural: 'He gave me ten rupees'.

---

## ⚡ Rule 11: Definite vs Indefinite Counting Nouns (Dozen, Hundred, Thousand...)
**Formula / Structure:** `Definite Number + Dozen/Hundred/Million + Noun (Singular) | Dozens/Hundreds + of + Noun (Plural)`

Words like dozen, score, hundred, thousand, million, billion remain SINGULAR when preceded by an exact number. They take '-s' and 'of' only when indefinite.

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *I bought two dozens bananas.*  
  ✔️ **I bought two dozen bananas.**
- ❌ *Three thousands people attended.*  
  ✔️ **Three thousand people attended.**

---

## ⚡ Rule 12: 'The + Adjective' Representing a Whole Class
**Formula / Structure:** `'The' + Adjective = Plural Common Noun + Plural Verb`

When 'the' is placed before adjectives like poor, rich, blind, deaf, educated, sick, it denotes all people in that category. It acts as a plural noun and takes a plural verb. Never add '-s' to the adjective.

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *The poors are honest.*  
  ✔️ **The poor are honest (Never say 'the poors').**
- ❌ *The rich exploits the poor.*  
  ✔️ **The rich exploit the poor.**

---

## ⚡ Rule 13: 'The + Plural Surname' Denoting the Whole Family
**Formula / Structure:** `'The' + Surname-s + Plural Verb`

Preceding a pluralized family name with 'The' refers to the entire family as a group, requiring a plural verb.

- **Example:** *The Sharmas have gone on vacation.* (Refers to the entire Sharma family.)
- **Example:** *The Guptas are our neighbors.* (Refers to all members of the Gupta family.)

## ⚡ Rule 15: Repeated Nouns Connected by a Preposition
**Formula / Structure:** `Singular Noun + Preposition + Same Singular Noun + Singular Verb`

When the same noun is repeated on either side of a preposition, both nouns MUST be in singular form, and the verb MUST be singular.

**⚠️ SSC Exam Traps (Spot the Error):**
- ❌ *Pages after pages were read.*  
  ✔️ **Page after page was read.**
- ❌ *Doors to doors were visited.*  
  ✔️ **Door to door was visited.**
- ❌ *Rows upon rows of marble looks beautiful.*  
  ✔️ **Row upon row of marble looks beautiful.**

---

## ⚡ Rule 16: Common Gender Nouns & Default Pronoun
**Formula / Structure:** `Common Gender Noun (Teacher, Doctor, Student, Clerk, Leader) -> Default Pronoun 'He/His'`

When the gender of a common noun is unknown or unspecified, traditional grammatical convention uses the masculine third-person pronoun (he/his/him), NOT 'it' or 'they'.

- **Example:** *Every student must submit his assignment on time.* (Use 'his', not 'their' or 'one's'.)

---

## ⚡ Rule 17: Noun Gender & Personified Gender Laws
Rules for masculine, feminine, and personified objects:

- **Adding '-ess' with vowel drops:** Actor → Actress, Hunter → Huntress, Waiter → Waitress, Master → Mistress
- **Personified Masculine (Strength, Violence, Power):** Sun, Summer, Winter, Time, Death, War
- **Personified Feminine (Beauty, Grace, Gentleness, Fertility):** Moon, Earth, Spring, Autumn, Nature, Liberty, Justice, Mercy, Peace, Ship

- **Example:** *The Sun shed his beams upon rich and poor alike.* (Sun = masculine ('his').)
- **Example:** *The Moon hid her face behind a cloud.* (Moon = feminine ('her').)

## ⚡ Rule 19: Singular to Plural Formation Traps & Compound Plurals
- 1. Nouns ending in 'o' preceded by vowel add 's' (Radio → Radios, Video → Videos, Bamboo → Bamboos).
- 2. Compound Nouns: Pluralize the HEAD / ROOT WORD, not the tail word:
-    • Sister-in-law → Sisters-in-law (NOT sister-in-laws)
-    • Commander-in-chief → Commanders-in-chief (NOT commander-in-chiefs)
-    • Passer-by → Passers-by (NOT passer-bys)
-    • Looker-on → Lookers-on
-    • Maid-servant → Maid-servants
-    • Step-son → Step-sons
-    • Man-servant → Men-servants (Both words change)
-    • Woman-doctor → Women-doctors

## ⚡ Rule 23: Comprehensive Apostrophe ('s) Laws
- i) Used for Living Beings: 'Boy's book', 'Cow's milk'. For Non-Living things, NEVER use 's — use 'of': 'The leg of the table' (NOT 'table's leg'), 'The cover of the book' (NOT 'book's cover').
- ii) Exceptions where Non-Living CAN take 's: Personified objects ('Nature's laws', 'Fortune's favorites'), Time/Space/Weight ('A day's leave', 'A mile's distance', 'A pound's weight'), Dignified celestial bodies ('The earth's surface', 'The sun's rays'), and established Idioms ('At one's wit's end', 'At an arm's length', 'At a stone's throw').
- iii) Nouns ending in 's' or hissing sound: Only add the apostrophe (') without 's': 'Keats' poems', 'Dickens' novels', 'For conscience' sake', 'For peace' sake', 'Boys' school'.
- iv) Joint Possession vs Separate Possession: If two nouns possess something jointly, add 's to the second noun only ('Ram and Shyam's father'). If they possess different items, add 's to both ('Ram's and Shyam's fathers').
- v) Compound Nouns: Apostrophe is added to the LAST word only: 'Father-in-law's house', 'Commander-in-chief's order' (NOT 'Father's-in-law').
- vi) Pronouns that NEVER take an Apostrophe: Possessive pronouns (hers, ours, yours, theirs, its, whose) NEVER take an apostrophe. 'Yours faithfully' (NOT 'Your's faithfully'). 'Its color' (possessive) vs 'It's' (= It is).
- vii) 'Else' following Indefinite Pronouns: When 'else' follows somebody, anybody, nobody, someone, anyone, put the apostrophe on 'else': 'Somebody else's book' (NOT 'Somebody's else').
