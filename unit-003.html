Unit 3

Present continuous and present simple 1I am doing and I do

Learn the difference between the present continuous (I am doing) and the present simple (I do) — and when to use each one.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the difference between present continuous and present simple
  • Use present continuous for actions happening now or around now
  • Use present simple for things in general, habits, and permanent situations
  • Recognize state verbs that are not normally used in continuous forms
  • Choose the correct tense in context

Part A — Theory

Study this example situation

David is in his car. He's driving to work.

He is driving — this is the present continuous. It means he is doing it now, at the time of speaking. The action is not finished.

But: David drives to work every day. This is the present simple. It means he does it regularly, as a habit.

Present continuous (I am doing)

We use the present continuous for actions happening now, at the time of speaking, or around now (temporary situations):

Please don't make so much noise. I'm trying to work. (not I try)

"Where's Tom?" "He's having a shower." (not He has a shower)

Let's go out now. It isn't raining any more. (not It doesn't rain)

I'm reading a really good book at the moment. (I started it but haven't finished it.)

Kate wants to work in Italy, so she's learning Italian.

Present simple (I do)

We use the present simple for things in general, things that happen all the time or repeatedly, or things that are true in general:

Nurses look after patients in hospitals.

I usually go away at weekends.

The earth goes round the sun.

The cafe opens at 7.30 in the morning.

Compare:

Present continuousPresent simple
I am doing (now, at the moment)I do (in general, repeatedly)
The water is boiling. Can you turn it off?Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
I'm living with friends until I find a flat.My parents live in London.
She is working hard today.She works hard every day.

State verbs (not normally used in continuous forms)

Some verbs describe states (not actions) and are not normally used in continuous forms:

likelove hatewant needprefer knowunderstand believeremember meanbelong ownseem appearcontain consistdepend

I want a cup of tea. (not I'm wanting)

Do you understand? (not Are you understanding?)

This book belongs to me. (not is belonging)

I know him well. (not I'm knowing)

Think / have / see / taste / smell

Some verbs have different meanings. Compare:

State meaning (simple only)Action meaning (can be continuous)
I think it's a good idea. (= I believe)I am thinking about it. (= I am considering)
We have a car. (= we own)We are having lunch. (= we are eating)
The soup tastes good. (= its flavour)I am tasting the soup. (= I am trying it)
I see what you mean. (= I understand)I am seeing the doctor tomorrow. (= I have an appointment)

He is selfish and He is being selfish

Compare these two sentences:

He is selfish. = He is a selfish person (his character).

He is being selfish. = He is behaving selfishly now (not his character, just his behaviour at the moment).

We don't use am being with state verbs: not I am being hungry / I am being tired
