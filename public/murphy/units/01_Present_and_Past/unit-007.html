Unit 7 • Tenses Basics

Present Perfect 1I have done

Learn how to use the present perfect to describe actions that happened in the past but have a direct result now, the difference between been and gone, and how to use just, already, and yet.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Form the present perfect (have/has + past participle).
  • Understand the connection between a past action and the present situation.
  • Distinguish between been to (visited and returned) and gone to (still there).
  • Use just, already, and yet correctly.

Part A — Theory

A. Present Action Connected with Now

Tom's Key

Tom is looking for his key. He can't find it.
He has lost his key.
He has lost his key = He lost it recently, and he still doesn't have it now.

The present perfect is have/has + past participle (lost, done, written, cleaned):

  • ‘Where's your passport?’ ‘I've lost it.’
  • He told me his name, but I've forgotten it. (I can't remember it now)
  • Is Sally here? ‘No, she's gone out.’ (she is out now)

B. been to vs gone to

gone to = he is there now

• James is on holiday. He has gone to Italy.
(= he is in Italy now or on his way there)

been to = he visited and has returned

• Jane is back home now. She has been to Italy.
(= she visited Italy, but she is back home now)

C. just, already, and yet

just = a short time ago

• ‘Are you hungry?’ ‘No, I've just had lunch.’

• Hello. Have you just arrived?

already = sooner than expected

• ‘Don't forget to pay the bill.’ ‘I've already paid it.’

• What time is Mark leaving? ‘He has already left.’

yet = until now (in questions and negatives)

• Has it stopped raining yet?

• I've written the email, but I haven't sent it yet.
