Unit 18 • Tenses Basics

used to (do)Past habits and states

Learn how to use used to for habits, routines, and states in the past that are no longer true today.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use used to + infinitive to talk about past habits and past states that are no longer true.
  • Recognize that used to exists only in the past (present: I do, not I use to).
  • Form questions with did you use to ...? and negatives with didn't use to ....

Part A — Theory

A. Study this example situation

Nicola doesn't travel much these days. She prefers to stay at home.
But she used to travel a lot. She used to go away two or three times a year.
She used to travel a lot = she travelled often in the past, but she doesn't do this any more.

I used to do something = I did it often in the past, but not any more:

  • I used to play tennis a lot, but I don't play very much now.
  • David used to spend a lot of money on clothes. These days he can't afford it.
  • 'Do you go to the cinema much?' 'Not now, but I used to.' (= I used to go)

We also use used to ... for things that were true, but are not true any more:

  • This building is now a furniture shop. It used to be a cinema.
  • I used to think Mark was unfriendly, but now I realise he's a very nice person.
  • I've started drinking coffee recently. I never used to like it before.
  • Lisa used to have very long hair when she was a child.

B. 'I used to do something' is past. There is no present.

You cannot say 'I use to do'. To talk about the present, we use the present simple (I do).

Past: used to

• he used to play

• we used to live

• there used to be

Present: present simple

• he plays

• we live

• there is

  • We used to live in a small village, but now we live in a city.
  • There used to be four cinemas in the town. Now there is only one.

C. Question and negative forms

The normal question form is did (you) use to ... ?:

  • Did you use to eat a lot of sweets when you were a child? (= did you do this often?)

The negative form is didn't use to ... (used not to ... is also possible):

  • I didn't use to like him. (or I used not to like him.)

D. Compare I used to do and I was doing

  • I used to watch TV a lot. (= I watched TV often in the past, but I don't do this any more)
  • I was watching TV when Rob called. (= I was in the middle of watching TV)

E. Do not confuse I used to do and I am used to doing

The structures and meanings are completely different:

  • I used to live alone = I lived alone in the past, but no longer.
  • I am used to living alone = It is familiar and normal for me (see Unit 61).

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 18

Category Rule Correct Example
used to + infinitive Past habit or state that is no longer true Nicola used to travel a lot, but she doesn't go away much these days.
No present form Use present simple for present habits (never 'I use to') We used to live in a village, but now we live in a city.
Questions and Negatives did you use to ... ? / didn't use to ... Did you use to play tennis? I didn't use to like coffee.
