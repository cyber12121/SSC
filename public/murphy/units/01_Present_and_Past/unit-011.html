Unit 11 • Tenses Basics

How long have you (been) ... ?Present perfect for ongoing duration

Learn how to form questions and answers with How long have you (been) ...? to describe ongoing states and activities that began in the past.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Ask and answer questions starting with How long have you (been) ...?.
  • Understand why we use the present perfect (not present simple) for an action starting in the past and continuing now.
  • Use live and work in both simple and continuous forms.

Part A — Theory

A. Period continuing up to now

Dan and Kate are married. They got married 20 years ago.
So they have been married for 20 years.
We say: They have been married for 20 years. (not They are married for 20 years)

We use the present perfect to say that something began in the past and is continuing now:

  • How long have you been in hospital? (not How long are you)
  • We've known each other for a long time. (not We know each other)
  • She has had a headache since she woke up.

B. live and work

With live and work, you can use the present perfect continuous OR simple:

• Julia has been living in this house for a long time. or Julia has lived in this house ...

• How long have you been working here? or How long have you worked here?

But use the continuous for temporary situations (I've been living with friends for a few days) and the simple with always (I've always lived here).
