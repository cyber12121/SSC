Unit 16 • Tenses Basics

Past Perfect ContinuousI had been doing

Learn how to use had been doing for activities in progress up to or just before a past moment in time.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Form the past perfect continuous: had been + -ing (had been raining, had been working).
  • Use it to talk about an action in progress up to or just before a past moment.
  • Distinguish between had been -ing (continuous) and had done (simple), and recognize non-continuous verbs.

Part A — Theory

A. Study this example situation

Yesterday morning I got up and looked out of the window. The sun was shining, but the ground was very wet.
It had been raining.

It was not raining when I looked out of the window. The sun was shining. But it had been raining before.

had been -ing is the past perfect continuous:

I / we / you / they / he / she / it + had been (= I'd been / he'd been etc.) + doing / working / playing etc.

Some more examples:

  • My hands were dirty because I'd been repairing my bike.
  • Tom was tired when he got home. He'd been working hard all day.
  • I went to Madrid a few years ago and stayed with a friend of mine. She hadn't been living there very long, but she knew the city very well.

You can say that something had been happening before something else happened:

  • We'd been playing tennis for about half an hour when it started to rain heavily.

B. Compare Present Perfect Continuous and Past Perfect Continuous

have been -ing (Present)

• I hope the bus comes soon. I've been waiting for 20 minutes. (before now)

• James is out of breath. He's been running. (= he has been ...)

had been -ing (Past)

• At last the bus came. I'd been waiting for 20 minutes. (before the bus came)

• James was out of breath. He'd been running. (= he had been ...)

C. Compare was -ing (Past Continuous) and had been -ing

  • It wasn't raining when we went out. The sun was shining. But it had been raining, so the ground was wet.
  • Katherine was lying on the sofa. She was tired because she'd been working hard.

D. Non-continuous verbs

Some verbs (for example, know) are not normally used in continuous forms (be + -ing):

  • We were good friends. We had known each other for years. (not had been knowing)
  • A few years ago Lisa cut her hair really short. I was surprised because she'd always had long hair. (not she'd been having)

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 16

Category Rule Correct Example
Past Perfect Continuous had been + -ing (had been raining) The ground was wet. It had been raining.
Duration before past moment Emphasizes how long an activity had been happening We'd been playing for half an hour when it started to rain.
State Verbs Exception Use past perfect simple (had known, had had) We had known each other for years (never had been knowing).
