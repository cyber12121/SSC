Unit 15 • Tenses Basics

Past PerfectI had done

Learn how to use the past perfect (had done) to talk about actions that happened before a specific time in the past.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Understand and form the past perfect: had + past participle (had gone, had seen).
  • Use the past perfect to talk about things that happened before another time/event in the past.
  • Compare the past perfect with the present perfect and the past simple.

Part A — Theory

A. Study this example situation

Sarah and Paul went to the same party last week, but they didn't see each other. Paul left the party at 10.30 and Sarah arrived at 11 o'clock.
So when Sarah arrived at the party, Paul wasn't there.
He had gone home.

had gone is the past perfect (simple):

I / we / they / you / he / she / it + had (= I'd / he'd etc.) + gone / seen / finished etc.

Sometimes we talk about something that happened in the past: Sarah arrived at the party. This is the starting point of the story. Then, if we want to talk about things that happened before this time, we use the past perfect (had ...):

  • When Sarah arrived at the party, Paul had already gone home.
  • When we got home last night, we found that somebody had broken into the flat.
  • Karen didn't come to the cinema with us. She'd already seen the movie.
  • At first I thought I'd done the right thing, but I soon realised that I'd made a big mistake.
  • The people sitting next to me on the plane were nervous. They hadn't flown before. (or They'd never flown before.)

B. Compare Present Perfect and Past Perfect

Present perfect (have / has seen)

• Who is that woman? I've seen her before, but I can't remember where.

• We aren't hungry. We've just had lunch.

• The house is dirty. They haven't cleaned it for weeks.

Past perfect (had seen)

• I wasn't sure who she was. I'd seen her before, but I couldn't remember where.

• We weren't hungry. We'd just had lunch.

• The house was dirty. They hadn't cleaned it for weeks.

C. Compare Past Simple and Past Perfect

Past simple (left, was)

• A: Was Tom there when you arrived?
B: Yes, but he left soon afterwards.

• Kate wasn't at home when I phoned. She was at her mother's house.

Past perfect (had left, had been)

• A: Was Tom there when you arrived?
B: No, he'd already left.

• Kate had just got home when I phoned. She'd been at her mother's house.

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 15

Category Rule Correct Example
Past Perfect Form had + past participle (had gone, had seen) When Sarah arrived, Paul had already gone home.
Time Reference An action earlier than another past action The film had already started when we arrived.
Past Simple vs Perfect Sequence of actions vs action completed before I felt tired, so I went to bed (sequence). Everybody had gone to bed (earlier).
