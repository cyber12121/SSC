Unit 12 • Tenses Basics

for and since, when ... ? and how long ... ?Prepositions of duration and past time

Learn how to choose between for and since, and how to use When did ...? with the past simple versus How long has ...? with the present perfect.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the difference between for (period of time) and since (start of a period).
  • Compare When ...? (+ past simple) with How long ...? (+ present perfect).
  • Use ago with the past simple.
  • Use It's ... since ... construction.

Part A — Theory

A. for and since

for + a period of time

for six months, for two hours, for five days, for a long time, for ages

• Helen has been in Ireland for three days.

since + the start of a period

since Monday, since 9 o'clock, since 2010, since I arrived

• Helen has been in Ireland since Monday.

B. When ...? vs How long ...?

When ...? + Past Simple

• When did it start raining?

• It started raining two hours ago.

How long ...? + Present Perfect

• How long has it been raining?

• It has been raining for two hours.

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 12.1 8 items • for or since

Write for or since.

  1. Helen has been in Ireland Monday.
  2. Helen has been in Ireland three days.
  3. My aunt has lived in Australia 15 years.
  4. Tina has been in her new job January.
  5. Nobody lives in those houses. They have been empty many years.
  6. The weather is dry. It hasn't rained a few weeks ago.
  7. I've been waiting here 7 o'clock.
  8. I haven't seen Tom ages.
Exercise 12.2 6 items • When vs How long

Look at each answer and choose the right question: How long ...? or When ...?

  1. It is raining. (how long?)
  2. You have a car. (how long?)
  3. You are waiting. (how long?)
  4. Your course started. (when?)
  5. Anna arrived in London. (when?)
  6. You know each other. (how long?)
Exercise 12.3 8 items • Read situations and complete

Read the situations and complete the sentences.

  1. It's raining. It started raining two hours ago.
    It's been raining for two hours.
  2. We are waiting for the bus. We started waiting 20 minutes ago.
    We've been waiting for 20 minutes.
  3. Jack is unwell. He became unwell on Sunday.
    .
  4. Alex is married. Alex and her husband got married a year ago.
    .
  5. You have a headache. It started when you woke up.
    .
  6. The meeting started at 9 o'clock. It's still going on now.
    .
  7. You're working in a hotel. You started working there six months ago.
    .
  8. Kate is learning Japanese. She started classes a long time ago.
    .

Key Takeaways for Unit 12

Category Rule Correct Example
