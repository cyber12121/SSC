Unit 4 • Tenses Basics

Present Continuous and Present Simple 2I am doing & I do

Learn which verbs are not normally used in continuous tenses (state verbs), how to use think in both forms, the special meaning of is being, and sensory verbs like smell, taste, and see.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Identify verbs that describe states rather than actions (e.g. know, like, belong) and always use them in the present simple.
  • Distinguish between I think (having an opinion) and I'm thinking (actively considering).
  • Understand the difference between general personality (he is selfish) and temporary behavior (he is being selfish).
  • Correctly use verbs of sense and perception (see, hear, smell, taste, look, feel).

Part A — Theory

A. Verbs Not Normally Used in Continuous Forms (State Verbs)

We use continuous forms (I’m waiting, it’s raining etc.) for actions and happenings that have started but not finished.

Some verbs (for example, know and like) describe states, feelings, or mental conditions rather than actions. They are not normally used in continuous tenses. We do not say ✗ ‘I am knowing’ or ✗ ‘they are liking’. We say ‘I know’ and ‘they like’.

• I’m hungry. I want something to eat. (not I’m wanting)

• Do you understand what I mean?

• Anna doesn’t seem very happy right now.

The Verb “think”

The verb think can express either an opinion or an active mental process:

think = believe / opinion (Simple)

When think means ‘believe’ or ‘have an opinion’, we do not use the continuous:

• I think Mary is Canadian, but I’m not sure. (not I’m thinking)

• What do you think of my idea? (= what is your opinion?)

think = consider / active thought (Continuous)

When think means ‘consider’ or deliberate, the continuous is possible:

• I’m thinking about what happened. I often think about it.

• Nicky is thinking of giving up her job. (= she is currently considering it)

B. am / is / are being (Temporary Behavior)

You can say he’s being …, you’re being … etc. to describe how somebody is behaving right now (an active action or choice):

Behavior at the moment

• I can’t understand why he’s being so selfish. He isn’t usually like that.
(being selfish = behaving selfishly at this moment)

• ‘The path is icy. Don’t slip.’ ‘Don’t worry. I’m being very careful.’

is / are being = temporary behavior

He is acting this way right now:

• Why are you being so unreasonable? You’re normally very patient.

is / are = general personality / state

This is what he is like in general:

• He never thinks about other people. He’s very selfish.

• I don’t like to take risks. I’m a very careful person.

Important Constraint

We use am/is/are being only to describe behavior that someone can control. It is not used for health, feelings, or involuntary states:

• Sam is ill. (not is being ill)

• Are you tired? (not are you being tired)

C. see, hear, smell, taste, look, feel

We normally use the present simple (not continuous) with verbs of involuntary sensory perception (see, hear, smell, taste):

Sensory Verbs in the Present Simple

• Do you see that man over there? (not are you seeing)

• The room smells. Let’s open a window.

• This soup doesn’t taste very good.

look and feel (Both forms possible)

You can use either the present simple or continuous to say how somebody looks or feels right now:

• You look well today.   or   You’re looking well today.

• How do you feel now?   or   How are you feeling now?

But for routine or general feelings, use the present simple:

• I usually feel tired in the morning. (not I’m usually feeling)

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 4.1 • Put the verb into present continuous or present simple
  1. Do you want — want is a state of desire, never continuous.
  2. believes — believe expresses a mental state/conviction, not continuous.
  3. I don’t remember (or I do not remember / I can’t remember) — mental recollection.
  4. I’m using — ongoing temporary activity happening around now.
  5. I need — need is a permanent/stative requirement.
  6. consists — factual/permanent state (scientific fact).
  7. does he want — want cannot be continuous.
  8. is he looking — look at is an active physical action happening right now.
  9. Do you recognise — mental recognition, always present simple.
  10. I’m thinking — active consideration/deliberation of selling the car.
  11. do you think — asking for an opinion (= what is your belief/verdict?).
  12. he seems — appearance/impression, always present simple.
Exercise 4.2 • Use the words in brackets to make sentences
  1. You don’t seem very happy today. — seem is a state verb.
  2. I’m thinking. (or I am thinking) — active mental processing in progress right now.
  3. Who does this umbrella belong to? — belong expresses possession, never continuous.
  4. This smells good. — involuntary sensory perception of odor.
  5. Is anybody sitting there? — ongoing physical state/action right now.
  6. These gloves don’t fit me. — fit expresses size/suitability, stative.
Exercise 4.3 • Are the underlined verbs OK? Correct them
  1. OK — is thinking of means actively considering.
  2. I don’t believe it. — believe cannot take the continuous.
  3. OK (or I feel) — both feel and am feeling are accepted for current feelings.
  4. does it taste — sensory perception verb used in simple form.
  5. OK — think here means ‘believe/have an opinion’.
  6. do you see — sensory perception verb, use present simple.
  7. OK — thinking about represents active thought process in progress.
Exercise 4.4 • is/are being vs is/are
  1. he’s being — behaving selfishly right now (out of character).
  2. is / ’s — describing her general personality (she is a nice person).
  3. is being / ’s being — behaving nice to me specifically at this moment.
  4. are / ’re — emotional state, not an act of deliberate behavior.
  5. are you being — acting unreasonably right now (contrasting with normal patience).
  6. Are you — physical sensation/state of hunger, cannot use being.
