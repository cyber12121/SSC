Unit 45 • Passive

it is said that … he is said to … he is supposed to …Impersonal passive reporting and meanings of supposed to

Master news reporting structures (is said to, is believed to) and explore all uses of 'supposed to'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Transform impersonal passive reporting sentences (It is said that...) into personal ones (He is said to...).
  • Use the perfect infinitive (to have done) to report past events.
  • Distinguish the meanings of supposed to: reputation vs. obligation/expectation.
  • Use not supposed to for prohibitions and advisory warnings.

Part A — Theory

A. Impersonal Reporting Passives

Study this example situation:

George is very old. Nobody knows exactly how old he is, but:
‘It is said that he is 108 years old.’  OR  ‘He is said to be 108 years old.’
Both sentences mean: ‘People say that he is 108 years old.’

You can use these structures with a number of reporting verbs, especially:
alleged • believed • considered • expected • known • reported • thought • understood

Pattern 1: It is + verb-ed + that ...

It is said that she runs 10 miles a day.

It is believed that the boy is wearing blue jeans.

It is expected that the strike will end soon.

It is alleged that he stole a car.

It is reported that two people were injured.

Pattern 2: Subject + is/are + verb-ed + to ...

She is said to run 10 miles a day.

The boy is believed to be wearing blue jeans.

The strike is expected to end soon.

He is alleged to have stolen a car.

Two people are reported to have been injured.

B. Supposed to …

1. Supposed to = said to (reputation / what people say):

  • I want to see that film. It's supposed to be good. (= people say it's good)
  • Fireworks are supposed to have been invented in China.

2. Supposed to = intended, arranged or expected (often different from reality):

  • The plan is supposed to be a secret, but everybody seems to know about it.
  • What are you doing at work? You're supposed to be on holiday.
  • Our guests were supposed to come at 7.30, but they were late.
  • Jane was supposed to phone me last night, but she didn't.

3. Not supposed to = not allowed or not advised:

  • You're not supposed to park your car here. It's private parking only.
  • Joe is much better, but he's not supposed to exercise too hard.

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 45.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. The strike is expected to end soon. (example)
  2. Many people are reported to be homeless after the floods.
  3. The thieves are thought to have got (or gotten) in through a window in the roof.
  4. The driver (of the car) is alleged to have been driving (or to have driven) at 110 miles an hour.
  5. The building is reported to have been badly damaged by the fire.
  6. The company is said to be losing a lot of money.
  7. The company is believed to have lost a lot of money last year.
  8. The company is expected to make a loss this year.
Exercise 45.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. it's supposed to be (example)
  2. they're / they are supposed to be — present belief.
  3. it's / it is supposed to have been — past state belief.
  4. they're / they are supposed to have won — past win.
  5. the view is supposed to be (or the view's supposed to be)
  6. she's / she is supposed to be living — continuous state.
Exercise 45.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. It's supposed to be a secret. (example)
  2. You're / You are supposed to be my friend.
  3. I'm / I am supposed to be on a diet.
  4. It was supposed to be a joke.
  5. Or maybe it's / it is supposed to be a flower.
  6. You're / You are supposed to be working.
  7. It's / It is supposed to be open every day.
Exercise 45.4 — Key & Explanations
  1. 're not supposed to park (example)
  2. 're / are supposed to start — scheduled start time.
  3. was supposed to phone — unfulfilled past intention.
  4. aren't / 're not / are not supposed to put — safety prohibition.
  5. was supposed to depart — delayed schedule.
  6. isn't / 's not / is not supposed to lift — medical advice.
