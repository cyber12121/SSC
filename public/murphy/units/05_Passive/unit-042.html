Unit 42 • Passive

Passive 1 (is done / was done)Present simple and past simple passive

Learn the fundamentals of passive voice: when to use it, how to form it in present and past simple, and using 'by' for the agent.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Form the passive in the present simple (am/is/are + past participle) and past simple (was/were + past participle).
  • Transform active sentences into passive sentences when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant.
  • Use by correctly to introduce the agent when necessary.
  • Differentiate between transitive verbs that can be passive and intransitive verbs (e.g. disappear, die, fall) that cannot.

Part A — Theory

A. Active vs. Passive

Compare active and passive sentences:

Active Voice

My grandfather built this house in 1981.

It employs two hundred people.

We say what the subject does.

Passive Voice

This house was built in 1981 (by my grandfather).

Two hundred people are employed by the company.

We say what happens to the subject.

When to Use the Passive When we use the passive, who or what causes the action is often unknown or unimportant:
  • A lot of money was stolen in the robbery. (somebody stole it, but we don't know who)
  • Is this room cleaned every day? (it's not important who cleans it)

If we want to say who does or what causes the action, we use by:
This house was built by my grandfather. • Two hundred people are employed by the company.

B. Formation of the Passive

The passive is formed with be (am / is / are / was / were etc.) + past participle:

(be) done (be) cleaned (be) damaged (be) built (be) seen

The past participle often ends in -ed (cleaned / damaged / invited), but many common verbs are irregular (built / done / stolen / seen). See Appendix 1.

C. Present Simple and Past Simple Passive

Present Simple: am/is/are + past participle

Active: Somebody cleans this room every day.

Passive: This room is cleaned every day.

  • Many accidents are caused by careless driving.
  • I'm not invited to parties very often.
  • How is this word pronounced?
Past Simple: was/were + past participle

Active: Somebody cleaned this room yesterday.

Passive: This room was cleaned yesterday.

  • We were woken up by a loud noise during the night.
  • ‘Did you go to the party?’ ‘No, I wasn't invited.’
  • How much money was stolen in the robbery?

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 42.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. are caused (example) — present simple passive: fact.
  2. is made — Cheese is made from milk.
  3. was damaged — past event in a storm.
  4. are shown — regular fact about cinemas.
  5. were invited — past invitation.
  6. is found / 's found — present habitat fact.
  7. were overtaken — past continuous action context.
  8. are held — regular schedule: every four years.
  9. was injured — past event.
  10. is surrounded — permanent state.
  11. was sent — past delivery error.
  12. is owned — present ownership.
