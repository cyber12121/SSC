Unit 93 • Relative clauses

Relative clauses 2: with and without who/that/whichObject relative clauses, omitting pronouns, prepositions at the end

When who/that/which is the object of the verb in a relative clause, you can leave it out. Notice preposition placement at the end of clauses.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish between relative pronouns as subject (mandatory) and as object (can be omitted).
  • Identify when who/that/which can be left out (the keys you lost, the woman I wanted to see).
  • Place prepositions at the end of relative clauses (the hotel you told me about, the job I applied for).
  • Use that (or no pronoun) after all, everything, nothing, the only (never use what).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Subject vs Object Pronouns (Leaving Out Who/That/Which)

Compare these two sentences:

Subject (CANNOT leave out)

The woman who lives next door is a doctor.
(‘who’ is the subject: she lives next door)

Where is the cheese that was in the fridge?
(‘that’ is the subject: it was in the fridge)

You must keep who/that/which when it is the subject.

Object (CAN leave out)

The woman (who/that) I wanted to see was away.
(‘I’ is subject; ‘woman’ is object: I wanted to see her)

Did you find the keys (that/which) you lost?
(‘you’ is subject; ‘keys’ is object: you lost them)

When it is the object, you can leave it out!

Part B: Prepositions in Relative Clauses

Notice the position of prepositions (to, with, for, about etc.) in relative clauses:

The woman I was talking to. (I was talking to her)
The music we were listening to.
The hotel you told me about.
The job she applied for.
The person you can rely on.

In spoken English, the relative pronoun is usually left out, and the preposition stays at the end of the clause.

Part C: All / Everything / Nothing + That (Never What)

After words like all, everything, something, nothing, anything, the only and superlatives, we use that (or leave it out), but never what:

Everything (that) they said was true. (not Everything what they said)
I gave her all the money (that) I had.
It was the best movie (that) I’ve ever seen.
