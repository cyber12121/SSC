Unit 96 • Relative clauses

Relative clauses 5: extra information clauses (2)Preposition + whom/which, all of / most of whom, which for a whole clause

In extra information clauses you can put prepositions before whom and which, use quantifiers (both of whom, most of which), and use which to refer to a whole sentence.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use prepositions before whom (people) and which (things) in Type 2 clauses: with whom, without which, of which.
  • Master quantifiers + of whom / of which: all of, most of, none of, neither of, both of.
  • Use which at the end of a sentence to refer to the entire preceding sentence/situation (Joe couldn't come, which was a shame).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Preposition + Whom / Which

In Type 2 extra-information clauses you can put a preposition before whom (for people) and which (for things):

Mr Lee, to whom I spoke on the phone, was very helpful.
Fortunately we had a good map, without which we would have got lost.
I share an office with my boss, with whom I get on really well.

Part B: All of / Most of etc. + Whom / Which

You can use whom and which after words like:

all of • most of • some of • none of • both of • neither of • each of • half of • one of • a few of
Helen has three brothers, all of whom are married.
They asked me a lot of questions, most of which I couldn’t answer.
My neighbours have two cars, one of which they never use.
Julia has two sisters, both of whom are lawyers.

Part C: 'Which' Referring to a Whole Sentence

We use which (not what) to refer back to the entire previous clause or fact:

Joe couldn’t come to the party, which was a shame.
(‘which’ = the fact that Joe couldn’t come)
The weather was very cold, which meant we couldn’t go swimming.
Amy was twenty minutes late, which she apologised for.
