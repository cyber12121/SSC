Unit 97 • Relative clauses

-ing and -ed clausesActive -ing participial clauses vs passive -ed participial clauses

We use -ing clauses to describe ongoing or active actions, and -ed clauses for passive meanings. These clauses are common after 'There is / There was'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master participial clauses: -ing clauses for active meanings (the woman talking to Tom = who is talking to Tom).
  • Master -ed clauses for passive meanings (the boy injured in the accident = who was injured).
  • Identify -ed forms of irregular verbs: made, stolen, blown, left.
  • Use There is / There was with -ing and -ed clauses (There was nobody injured; There's somebody coming).

Part A — Theory

Part A: -ing Clauses (Active Meaning)

We can use an -ing clause to say what somebody or something is doing (or was doing) at a particular time:

Do you know the woman talking to Tom?
(= the woman who is talking to Tom)
Police investigating the crime are looking for three men.
(police who are investigating)
I was woken up by a bell ringing.
(= a bell that was ringing)

Part B: -ed Clauses (Passive Meaning)

-ed clauses have a passive meaning (completed action or condition):

The boy injured in the accident was taken to hospital.
(= the boy who was injured in the accident)
George showed me some pictures painted by his father.
(= pictures that were painted by his father)
Most of the suggestions made at the meeting were not practical.

Part C: There is / There was + -ing / -ed

We often use -ing and -ed clauses after There is / There was etc.:

There were some children swimming in the river.
Is there anybody waiting?
There was nobody injured in the crash.
There's nothing left.
