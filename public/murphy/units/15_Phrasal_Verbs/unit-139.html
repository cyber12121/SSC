Unit 139 • Phrasal verbs

Phrasal verbs 3: outout = not burning/shining, work out, and other verbs with out

Learn phrasal verbs with out: putting out fires, exercising, calculating, progress, carrying out orders, and sorting out problems.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use out for extinguishing fires, candles, and lights (go out, put out, turn out, blow out).
  • Master the multiple meanings of work out: do exercise, develop/progress, calculate, understand/solve.
  • Use carry out (orders, surveys), point out (draw attention to), and sort out (resolve problems).
  • Use find out (learn information), turn out (result/become clear), give/hand out (distribute), run out (deplete), and try out (test).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Out = Not Burning, Not Shining

We use out when talking about flames, candles, and lights ceasing to burn or shine:

go out: Suddenly all the lights in the building went out.
put out a fire / cigarette / light: I put the fire out with a fire extinguisher.
turn out a light: I turned the lights out before leaving.
blow out a candle: We don’t need the candle. You can blow it out.

Part B: The Many Meanings of Work Out

work out is one of the most versatile phrasal verbs in English:

1. Do physical exercise:
Rachel works out at the gym three times a week.
2. Develop, progress well:
Good luck for the future. I hope everything works out well for you.
Things didn’t work out. (= didn’t develop successfully)
3. Calculate:
The total bill for three people is £97.35. That works out at £32.45 each.
345 × 76? I need a calculator. I can’t work it out in my head.
4. Understand / find an answer (= figure out):
Investigators are trying to work out what caused the accident.  or  ... trying to figure out...

Part C: Other Important Verbs with Out

carry out an order / an experiment / a survey / an investigation / a plan = perform it:
Soldiers are expected to carry out orders. / An investigation will be carried out.
point something out (to somebody) = draw attention to a fact or detail:
As we drove through the city, the guide pointed out all the sights. / Somebody pointed it out to me.
sort something out = find a solution to a problem, put in order:
There are a few problems we need to sort out. / All these papers are mixed up; I’ll sort them out.
find out = get information / discover facts:
The police never found out who committed the crime. / I just found out it’s Helen’s birthday.
turn out = end up being / prove to be:
Nobody believed Paul, but he turned out to be right. / The weather turned out nice later.
give/hand out = distribute to each person:
At the end of the lecture, the speaker gave out information sheets.
run out (of something) = have none left after using it all:
We ran out of petrol on the motorway.
try out = test a machine, system, or idea to see if it works well:
The company is trying out some new software at the moment.
