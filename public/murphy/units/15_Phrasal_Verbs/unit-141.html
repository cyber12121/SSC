Unit 141 • Phrasal verbs

Phrasal verbs 5: on/off (2)verb + on = continue; verb + off (finish off, tell off, rip off, show off)

Master verb + on for continuation (go on, keep on, drive on) and special off verbs including doze off, tell off, rip off, and show off.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Understand verb + on to mean continue doing something (go on, carry on, keep on, drive on, walk on).
  • Master get on for progress (How are you getting on?), relationships (get on with), and resumption of work.
  • Use special off verbs: doze off / drop off / nod off (fall asleep), go off (alarms), finish off, tell off (reprimand), rip off (overcharge/cheat), show off (boast), and put off (discourage).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Verb + On = Continue Doing Something

Adding on to many verbs means to continue an action without stopping:

go on = continue:
The party went on until 4 o’clock in the morning.
go on / carry on doing something = continue doing it:
We can’t go on spending money like this. We’ll have nothing left soon.
I don’t want to carry on working here. I’m going to look for another job.
go on with / carry on with something = continue an activity:
Don’t let me disturb you. Please carry on with what you’re doing.
keep on doing (or keep doing) = do continuously or repeatedly:
He keeps on criticising me. It’s not fair!  (or He keeps criticising me.)
drive on / walk on / play on:
Shall we stop at this petrol station or shall we drive on to the next one?

Part B: Meanings of Get On

1. get on = progress / manage:
How are you getting on in your new job? (= How is it going?)
2. get on (with somebody) = have a good relationship:
Joanne and Karen don’t get on. They’re always arguing.
Richard gets on well with his neighbours. They’re all very friendly.
3. get on with something = continue something you have to do:
I must get on with my work. I have a lot to do.

Part C: Verb + Off — Special Idiomatic Meanings

doze off / drop off / nod off = fall asleep unexpectedly:
I dozed off during the lecture. It was very boring.
go off = make an alarm or bell ring:
Did you hear the alarm go off?
show off = boast or try to impress others with ability/knowledge:
Look at that boy riding with no hands. He’s just showing off.
tell somebody off = scold or speak angrily to somebody because they did wrong:
Clare’s mother told her off for wearing dirty shoes in the house.
finish something off = complete the final part of it:
Have you finished painting the kitchen? — Nearly. I’ll finish it off tomorrow.
rip somebody off / be ripped off = cheat somebody / charge far too much:
Did you really pay £2,000 for that painting? I think you were ripped off.
put somebody off (doing) something = discourage somebody from doing it:
We wanted to go to the exhibition, but we were put off by the long queue.
