Unit 143 • Phrasal verbs

Phrasal verbs 7: up (1)Approaching, starting, completing, and managing

Learn key verbs with up: come up to, grow up, bring up, take up, use up, fix up, catch up, and give up.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use verbs with up for approaching: go up to, come up to, walk up to.
  • Master verbs for beginning, growing, and raising: set up (start organisation/business), grow up (become adult), bring up (raise child).
  • Master verbs for cleaning and full consumption: clean up, clear up, tidy up, wash up, use up.
  • Use verbs for time, hobbies, and arrivals: take up (space/time/hobby), fix up (meeting), turn up / show up (arrive).
  • Master progress and outcomes: catch up with, keep up with, end up, make up / be made up of, give up.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Approaching — Go Up To, Come Up To, Walk Up To

go up, come up, walk up (to somebody/something) = approach a person or place:

A man came up to me in the street and asked me for money.
Kate walked up to the front door of the house and rang the doorbell.

Part B: Starting, Growing, Raising

set up an organisation, company, business, system, website = start it:
The government has set up a committee to investigate the problem.
Helen has her own website. A friend helped her to set it up.
grow up = become an adult:
Amy was born in Hong Kong but grew up in Australia.
What do you want to be when you grow up?
bring up a child = raise and care for during childhood:
Her parents died when she was a child and she was brought up by her grandparents.

Part C: Cleaning, Tidying, and Using Completely

clean up / clear up / tidy up = make clean and orderly:
Look at this mess! Who’s going to tidy up?  (or ... to tidy it up)
wash up = wash plates and dishes after a meal:
After dinner I washed up and put the dishes away.
use something up = use all of it until nothing is left:
I’m going to make soup. We have a lot of vegetables and I want to use them up.
I’ve used up all my energy.

Part D: Space, Time, Appointments, and Arrivals

take up a hobby, sport, activity = start doing it:
Laura took up photography a few years ago. She takes great pictures.
take up space or time = occupy / use:
Most of the space was taken up by a large table. / Gardening takes up most of my time.
turn up / show up = arrive, appear:
We arranged to meet David last night, but he didn’t turn up.
fix up a meeting etc. = arrange, organise:
We’ve fixed up a meeting for next Monday.

Part E: Pacing, Endings, and Quitting

catch up (with somebody) = move faster to reach someone ahead:
You go on and I’ll catch up with you.
keep up (with somebody) = continue at the same speed/level:
You’re walking too fast. I can’t keep up with you. / You’re doing well. Keep it up!
end up somewhere / doing something = reach a situation in the end:
Three men ended up in hospital. / I couldn’t find a hotel and ended up sleeping on a bench.
make up / be made up of = constitute, consist of:
Children make up half the population. / Air is made up mainly of nitrogen and oxygen.
give up = stop trying; give something up = quit doing it:
Don’t give up. Keep trying! / Sue got bored with her job and decided to give it up.
