Unit 145 • Phrasal verbs

Phrasal verbs 9: away/backComparing away and back, plus other verbs with away and back

Understand movements away and back, returning items, keeping away, getting away with things, and paying back.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Compare directional meanings of away (away from home, a person, or a place) and back (returning home or to a place/person).
  • Use common away verbs: drive away, fly away, blow away, take away, walk/run/look away.
  • Master other verbs with away: keep away (avoid approaching), give away (donate/gift), put away (store), throw away (discard), get away (escape), get away with (avoid punishment).
  • Master other verbs with back: wave/smile/shout back (reciprocate), call/phone/ring back, get back to (reply), look back on (reflect), pay back (repay money).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Comparing Away and Back

away = away from home / place / person

away = away from home:
We’re going away on holiday today.

away = away from a place, person etc.:
Sarah got into her car, started the engine and drove away.
I tried to take a picture of the bird, but it flew away.
I dropped the ticket, and it blew away in the wind.
The police searched the house and took away a computer.

Also: walk away, run away, look away.

back = returning home / to a place or person

back = back home:
We’ll be back in three weeks.

back = back to a place, a person etc.:
‘I’m going out now.’ ‘What time will you be back?’
After eating at a restaurant, we walked back to our hotel.
I’ve still got Jane’s keys. I forgot to give them back to her.
When you’ve finished with that book, can you put it back on the shelf?

Also: go back, come back, get back, take something back.

Part B: Other Verbs with Away

keep away (from ...) = don’t go near / maintain distance:
Keep away from the edge of the pool. You might fall in.
give something away = give to somebody else free because you don’t need it any more:
‘Did you sell your bike?’ ‘No, I gave it away to a friend.’
put something away = put it in the place where it is usually kept:
When the children finished playing with their toys, they put them away.
throw something away = put it in the rubbish / discard:
I kept the letter, but I threw away the envelope.
get away = escape, leave with difficulty:
We tried to catch the thief, but she got away.
get away with something = do something wrong without being caught or punished:
I parked in a no-parking zone, but I got away with it. I didn’t have to pay a fine.

Part C: Other Verbs with Back

wave back / smile back / shout back / hit somebody back = return the action:
I waved to her, and she waved back.
call / phone / ring (somebody) back = return a phone call:
I can’t talk to you now. I’ll call you back in ten minutes.
get back to somebody = reply to them by phone, email, or message:
I sent him an email, but he never got back to me.
look back (on something) = think about what happened in the past:
Looking back on it, I learnt a lot and it was a useful experience.
pay back money / pay somebody back = return borrowed money:
If you borrow money, you have to pay it back.
Thanks for lending me the money. I’ll pay you back next week.
