Unit 57 • -ing and to ...

Verb + -ing or to … 2try, need, help

Master the critical distinctions with try (try to do vs try doing), need (need to do vs passive needs doing), and expressions with help (help do and can't help doing).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between try to do (make an effort / attempt) and try -ing (experiment / test a solution).
  • Distinguish active need to do from the passive meaning of needs -ing (e.g. My phone needs charging).
  • Use help to do / help do with or without to.
  • Apply the idiomatic expression can't help doing (= can't stop oneself from doing).

Part A — Theory

A. try to … vs. try -ing

try to do (attempt / make an effort)

try to do = attempt to do, make an effort to do something difficult:

• I was very tired. I tried to keep my eyes open, but I couldn’t.
• Please try to be quiet when you come home.
• I tried to move the table, but it was too heavy. (so I couldn't move it)
try doing (experiment / test)

try something / try -ing = do it as an experiment or test to see if it solves a problem:

• These cakes are delicious. You should try one.
• The photocopier doesn’t seem to be working. ➔ Try pressing the green button.
• I didn't like the furniture arrangement, so I tried moving the table to the other side.

B. need to … vs. need -ing

I need to do something = it is necessary for me to do it (active):

  • He needs to work harder if he wants to make progress.
  • I don’t need to come to the meeting, do I?
Passive Meaning: needs -ing Something needs -ing = it needs to be done:
• My phone needs charging. (= it needs to be charged)
• Does your suit need cleaning? (= need to be cleaned)
• It’s a difficult problem. It needs thinking about carefully.

C. help and can’t help

You can say help to do or help do (with or without to):

  • Everybody helped to clean up after the party. (or helped clean up)
  • Can you help me move this table? (or help me to move)
Idiom: can’t help doing (= cannot stop myself) • I don’t like him, but I can’t help feeling sorry for him.
• She tried to be serious, but she couldn’t help laughing.
• I’m sorry I’m so nervous. I can’t help it. (= can't help being nervous)
