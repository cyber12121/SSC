Unit 56 • -ing and to ...

Verb + -ing or to … 1remember, regret etc.

Master verbs that can be followed by either -ing or to … with a crucial difference in meaning (remember, regret, go on) versus verbs where both forms are interchangeable.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the difference between remember doing (retrospective memory) and remember to do (task to be completed).
  • Distinguish between regret doing (sorry about a past deed) and regret to say/tell (formal apology for delivering news).
  • Contrast go on doing (continue same action) with go on to do (advance to a new stage).
  • Recognize neutral verbs where -ing and to … share identical meaning: begin, start, continue, intend, bother.

Part A — Theory

A. Meaning Differences: Remember, Regret, Go on

Some verbs change meaning depending on whether they are followed by -ing or to …:

remember doing / regret doing

I remember doing something = I did it and now I remember this (action happened first):

• I clearly remember locking the door.
• He could remember driving just before the crash.

I regret doing something = I did it and now I am sorry about it:

• I now regret saying what I said.
remember to do / regret to say

I remembered to do something = I remembered that I had to do it, so I did it:

• I remembered to lock the door, but forgot the windows.
• Remember to buy some bananas.

I regret to say / tell / inform = I am sorry that I have to deliver this news:

• I regret to say that we cannot accept your offer.
Go on doing vs. Go on to do • go on doing = continue doing the same thing:
  The president paused for a moment and then went on talking.
• go on to do = do or say something new:
  After discussing the economy, the president went on to talk about foreign policy.

B. Verbs with No Difference in Meaning

These verbs can be followed by -ing or to … with no difference of meaning:

begin start continue intend bother
  • It started raining. or It started to rain.
  • Andy intends buying a house. or Andy intends to buy a house.
  • Don’t bother locking the door. or Don’t bother to lock …
Avoid -ing after -ing Normally we do not use -ing directly after -ing:
It’s starting to rain. (not usually It's starting raining)
