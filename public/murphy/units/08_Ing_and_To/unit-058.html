Unit 58 • -ing and to ...

Verb + -ing or to … 3like / would like etc.

Master nuances with like, love, hate, conditional forms with would like, and retrospective desires with would like to have done.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use both -ing and to … after like, love, hate for repeated activities.
  • Recognize when -ing is required (existing situations: He likes living there).
  • Contrast I like doing (I enjoy it) with I like to do (I choose it / think it is sensible).
  • Use to … after would like / would love / would hate / would prefer.
  • Form retrospective desires with would like to have done (regretting an unfulfilled wish).

Part A — Theory

A. like / love / hate

When you talk about repeated actions, you can use -ing or to …:

  • Do you like getting up early? or Do you like to get up early?
  • Stephanie hates flying. or Stephanie hates to fly.
  • I love meeting people. or I love to meet people.
Special Rules 1. Existing situations: Use -ing only when referring to a situation that already exists:
• Paul lives in Berlin now. He likes living there.
• Do you like being a student?

2. Enjoyment vs Choice:
• I like cleaning the kitchen = I enjoy it.
• I like to clean the kitchen = I choose to do it as a good habit, whether I enjoy it or not.

B. would like / would love / would hate / would prefer

Conditional forms with would are usually followed by to … (infinitive):

• I’d like to go away for a few days.
• What would you like to do this evening?
• I wouldn’t like to go on holiday alone.
• I’d love to meet your family.

Compare:
• I like playing tennis. / I like to play tennis. (= general enjoyment)
• I’d like to play tennis today. (= specific wish for now)

C. would like to have done (Retrospective Desires)

I would like to have done something = I regret now that I didn’t or couldn’t do it:

  • It’s a shame we didn’t see Anna. I would like to have seen her again.
  • We’d like to have gone away, but we were too busy at home.
  • Poor David! I would hate to have been in his position.
  • I’d love to have gone to the party, but it was impossible.
