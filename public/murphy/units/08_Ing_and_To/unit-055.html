Unit 55 • -ing and to ...

Verb (+ object) + to …I want you to …

Understand verbs that require or allow an object before the infinitive (tell somebody to do), contrasted with bare infinitive verbs (make somebody do, let somebody do).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between verb + to … and verb + object + to … (e.g. I want to go vs I want you to go).
  • Recognize verbs that require an object: tell, advise, remind, warn, invite, encourage, persuade, get, force, teach, allow, enable.
  • Avoid the common error with *suggest*: never say suggest somebody to do.
  • Master bare infinitives with make and let (make somebody do, let somebody do), and the passive shift (be made to do).

Part A — Theory

A. Verb + to … vs. Verb + Object + to …

Some verbs can be used with or without an object:

Verb + to …
• We expected to be late.
• Would you like to go now?
• He doesn’t want to know.
Verb + Object + to …
• We expected Dan to be late.
• Would you like me to go now?
• He doesn’t want anybody to know.
Important Watch-out We do not usually say 'want that':
• Do you want me to come with you? (not want that I come)

B. Verbs with the Structure: Verb + Object + to …

These verbs usually require an object before the infinitive:

tell
advise
remind
warn
invite
encourage
persuade
get
force
teach
allow
enable
  • It’s not a nice hotel. I wouldn’t advise you to stay there.
  • Can you remind me to call Sam tomorrow?
  • Joe warned me not to touch the switch.
  • I didn’t move the piano by myself. I got somebody to help me.
  • They don’t allow people to park in front of the building.
Never with Suggest Jane suggested that I ask you for advice. (not Jane suggested me to ask)

C. Make and Let (Bare Infinitive: Without To)

We say make somebody do and let somebody do (without to):

  • I made him promise that he wouldn’t tell anybody. (not made him to promise)
  • Hot weather makes me feel tired. (= causes me to feel tired)
  • Her parents wouldn’t let her go out alone. (= wouldn’t allow her to go out)
  • Let me carry your bag for you.
Passive Shift: (be) made to do In the passive voice, make takes to:
We were made to wait for two hours. (= They made us wait …)
