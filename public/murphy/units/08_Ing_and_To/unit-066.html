Unit 66 • -ing and to ...

to … (afraid to do) and preposition + -ingafraid of -ing / interested in -ing / sorry for -ing

Master subtle distinctions between infinitive and preposition + gerund: afraid to do vs afraid of doing, interested in doing vs interested to hear, and sorry for doing vs sorry to say.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between afraid to do (deliberate avoidance due to danger) and afraid of something happening (fear of accidental consequence).
  • Distinguish between interested in doing (considering an ambition/activity) and interested to hear/know (finding information intriguing).
  • Contrast sorry for doing (apologising for an action) with sorry to hear/disturb (regret or politeness).
  • Review broad verb + infinitive vs verb + preposition + -ing patterns.

Part A — Theory

A. afraid to do vs. afraid of -ing

afraid to do (unwilling to act)

I don’t want to do it because it is dangerous or the result could be bad:

• This street is dangerous. People are afraid to walk here at night.
• James was afraid to tell his parents what had happened.
• I was afraid to go near the dog...
afraid of -ing (fear of consequence)

I am afraid that an unpleasant thing will happen accidentally:

• The path was icy. We were afraid of falling. (not afraid to fall)
• I don’t like dogs. I’m always afraid of being bitten.
• ...because I was afraid of being bitten.

B. interested in -ing vs. interested to do

1. interested in doing = I’m thinking of doing it, I would like to do it:
• Let me know if you’re interested in joining the club. (not to join)
• Nobody was interested in buying the car.

2. interested to hear / see / know = it was interesting for me:
• I was interested to hear that Tanya left her job. (= I heard this and found it interesting)
• I would be interested to know what Mike thinks.

C. sorry for … vs. sorry to …

1. sorry for (doing) = apologise for a past deed:
• I’m sorry for shouting at you yesterday. (or I'm sorry I shouted)

2. sorry to … = regret what happens or apologise at the moment of acting:
• I’m sorry to hear that Nicky lost her job.
• I’ve enjoyed my stay. I’ll be sorry to leave.
• I’m sorry to bother you, but I need to ask a question.
