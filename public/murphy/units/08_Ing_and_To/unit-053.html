Unit 53 • -ing and to ...

Verb + -ingenjoy doing / stop doing etc.

Master the verbs and phrasal verbs that are strictly followed by -ing (gerund), structures with somebody + -ing, and perfect gerund forms (having done).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Recognize verbs strictly followed by -ing: enjoy, mind, suggest, stop, finish, consider, avoid, admit, deny, risk, imagine, fancy.
  • Form negatives correctly with not -ing (e.g. enjoy not having to get up early).
  • Use phrasal verbs + -ing: give up, put off, go on / carry on, keep / keep on.
  • Apply the pattern verb + somebody + -ing and understand finished actions with having done.

Part A — Theory

A. Verbs Followed by -ing (Not to …)

After verbs like enjoy, mind and suggest, we use -ing (not to …):

Key Rule • I enjoy reading. (not I enjoy to read)
• Would you mind closing the door? (not mind to close)
• Chris suggested going to the cinema. (not suggested to go)

  • Suddenly everybody stopped talking. There was silence.
  • I’ll do the shopping when I’ve finished cleaning the flat.
  • He tried to avoid answering my question.
  • I don’t fancy going out this evening. (= I’m not enthusiastic about it)
  • Have you ever considered going to live in another country?
  • They said they were innocent. They denied doing anything wrong.
Negative Form: not -ing When I’m on holiday, I enjoy not having to get up early.

B. Phrasal Verbs Followed by -ing

We also use -ing after prepositional and phrasal verbs:

  • give up (= stop): I’ve given up buying newspapers. I don’t read them any more.
  • put off (= delay until later): You shouldn’t put off telling him what happened. You need to tell him now.
  • go on or carry on (= continue): Katherine doesn’t want to retire. She wants to go on working. (or … carry on working)
  • keep or keep on (= do continuously/repeatedly): You keep interrupting when I’m talking. (or You keep on interrupting …)

C. Verb + Somebody + -ing & Finished Actions (having done)

With some verbs you can use the structure verb + somebody + -ing:

  • You can’t stop people doing what they want.
  • I can’t imagine George riding a motorbike.
  • Did she really say that? I don’t remember her saying that.
  • Sorry to keep you waiting so long.
Finished Actions: having done When talking about finished actions, you can use having done/stolen/said, though simple -ing is also common:
• They admitted having stolen the money. (or … admitted stealing the money)
• I now regret saying that. (or … regret having said that)
