Unit 61 • -ing and to ...

be/get used to …I'm used to …

Master the essential difference between past habits with used to do and familiarity with be/get used to doing, where to is a preposition followed by -ing or a noun.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Understand the progression: wasn't used to it ➔ got used to it ➔ is used to it.
  • Recognize that in be/get used to, to is a preposition requiring -ing or a noun (used to living alone, not used to live).
  • Distinguish clearly between I used to do (past habit, no longer true) and I am used to doing (familiar, not strange).

Part A — Theory

A. Study this Example Situation: Lisa in Britain

Lisa is American, but she lives in Britain. When she first drove a car in Britain, driving on the left was a problem for her because:

• She wasn’t used to it.
• She wasn’t used to driving on the left. (because Americans drive on the right)
• But after some time, driving on the left became easier. She got used to driving on the left.
• Now it’s no problem for Lisa: She is used to driving on the left.

I’m used to something = it is not new or strange for me:

  • Paul lives alone. He has lived alone for a long time, so he is used to living alone.
  • I bought some new shoes. They felt strange at first because I wasn’t used to them.
  • Our new apartment is noisy. I expect we’ll get used to the noise.
  • Helen isn't used to getting up so early.

B. Structure: be/get used to + -ing

In this structure, to is a preposition (not part of the infinitive):

Key Rule We say be/get used to doing something (not used to do):
• Lisa is used to driving on the left. (not is used to drive)
• I’m used to living alone. (not I'm used to live)
• We’re not used to the noise / it / living here.

C. Compare: I am used to doing vs. I used to do

I am used to doing (familiarity)

It is not strange or new for me (present or past state):

• I’m used to the weather here.
• I’m used to driving on the left because I’ve lived in Britain a long time.
I used to do (past habit)

I did it regularly in the past, but no longer do it:

• I used to drive to work every day, but these days I usually go by bike.
• We used to live outside the town, but now we live near the centre.
