Unit 67 • -ing and to ...

see somebody do and see somebody doingVerbs of Perception

Master sensory verbs of perception (see, hear, listen to, watch, feel, smell, find): complete completed actions (see somebody do) versus ongoing actions in progress (see somebody doing).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between see somebody do (complete action witnessed from start to finish) and see somebody doing (action witnessed in progress).
  • Apply the bare infinitive rule with hear, listen to, watch, feel.
  • Apply the participle -ing pattern with hear, listen to, watch, feel, smell, find.
  • Recognize when either form is acceptable (e.g. I've never seen her dance / dancing).

Part A — Theory

A. Two Structures: see somebody do vs. see somebody doing

Study these contrasting situations:

see somebody do (Complete Action)

I saw him do something = he did it and I saw the complete action from start to finish:

• Tom got into his car and drove off. ➔ I saw Tom get into his car and drive off.
• He jumped over the wall and ran away. ➔ I saw him jump over the wall and run away.
• They went out. ➔ I heard them go out.
see somebody doing (Action in Progress)

I saw him doing something = he was in the middle of doing it when I saw him:

• Kate was waiting for a bus. ➔ I saw Kate waiting for a bus.
• I saw Tom as I drove past. He was walking along the street. ➔ I saw Tom walking.
• Can you smell something burning?

B. Sensory Verbs that take these Structures

  • hear: I didn't hear you come in. / I could hear it raining.
  • listen to: Listen to the birds singing!
  • feel: Lisa suddenly felt somebody touch her on the shoulder. / I can feel something crawling up my leg.
  • smell: Can you smell something burning?
  • find: We found him sitting under a tree eating an apple.
