Unit 65 • -ing and to ...

Adjective + to …hard to understand, nice of you to … etc.

Master adjective + infinitive structures: easy/hard to do, personal evaluations with it was nice/silly of you to do, emotional reactions with sorry/surprised to hear, and predictive certainty with bound/sure/likely to happen.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Transform sentences between It is hard to understand him and He is hard to understand (without a redundant object).
  • Use it is nice / kind / silly / unfair of somebody to do.
  • Express reactions to news with adjective + to … (sorry/glad/surprised/pleased to hear).
  • Use to … after ordinal expressions: the first, the second, the last, the only (person to do).
  • Express probability and certainty: bound to, sure to, likely to.

Part A — Theory

A. hard to understand, interesting to talk to etc.

Compare sentences (a) and (b):

(a) Preparatory It
• It is hard to understand him.
• It was impossible to answer the exam questions.
• It’s interesting to talk to Nicola.
(b) Subject + Adjective + to …
• He is hard to understand. (not to understand him)
• The questions were impossible to answer.
• Nicola is interesting to talk to. (not to talk to her)

This structure applies to adjectives like easy, hard, difficult, impossible, dangerous, safe, expensive, cheap, nice, good, interesting, exciting.
Also with nouns: This is a difficult question to answer.

B. It’s nice of you to …

We use It is + adjective + of somebody + to … to comment on someone's actions:

• It was nice of you to take me to the airport. Thank you very much.
• It’s silly of Ruth to give up her job when she needs the money.
• I think it was unfair of him to criticise me.
Also: kind, generous, careless, stupid, inconsiderate, typical.

C. Reactions & Ordinals (the first to …)

1. Reactions: sorry to hear, glad to get, pleased to help, surprised to see, amazed to discover:
• I’m sorry to hear that your mother isn't well.

2. The first / second / last / only + to …:
• I was the only one to arrive on time.
• You will be the first to know.

3. bound to / sure to / likely to:
• Carla is a very good student. She’s bound to pass the exam. (= sure to pass)
• It’s possible I’ll win, but it’s not likely to happen. (= improbable)
