Unit 48 • Reported speech

Reported speech 2say vs. tell, reporting imperatives (tell/ask somebody to …), and situations still true

Deepen your understanding of reported speech: say vs. tell, reporting commands and requests with infinitives, and when backshift is optional.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Identify when tense backshifting is optional for persistent present truths.
  • Differentiate precisely between say and tell based on personal objects.
  • Report imperatives and commands using tell + person + to + infinitive.
  • Report requests and advice using ask / tell + person + (not) to + infinitive.

Part A — Theory

A. When Tense Backshift is Optional (Still True)

We do not always change the verb in reported speech. If the situation is still the same now, you can keep the present tense:

Direct Speech

Paul: ‘My new job is boring.’

Helen: ‘I want to go to Canada next year.’

Reported Speech (Still True)

Paul said that his new job is boring. (still boring now)

Helen told me that she wants to go to Canada. (still wants to)

You can also change the verb to the past: Paul said that his new job was boring. Both are correct.

When Past is Mandatory If the situation has changed or is finished, you MUST use the past:
• Paul left suddenly. He said he had to go. (NOT has to go)

You must also use the past when what was said is different from the truth:
• Rachel said: ‘Joe is in hospital.’ Later you meet Joe walking in the street. You say:
“Joe, this is a surprise! Rachel said you were in hospital.” (NOT you are in hospital – clearly he isn't!)

B. Say vs. Tell

If you say who somebody is talking to, use tell (with a personal object):

  • Rachel told me that you were in hospital. (NOT Rachel said me)
  • What did you tell the police? (NOT say the police)

Otherwise use say (no personal object):

  • Rachel said that you were in hospital. (NOT Rachel told that)
  • What did you say?

You can say ‘say something to somebody’:
Anna said goodbye to me and left. • What did you say to the police?

C. Reporting Imperatives: tell / ask somebody to do …

For orders, instructions, and requests, we use tell / ask somebody to do something:

Direct Order / Request

‘Drink plenty of water,’ the doctor said to me.

‘Don't work too hard,’ I said to Joe.

‘Can you help me, please?’ Jackie said to me.

Reported with to-Infinitive

The doctor told me to drink plenty of water.

I told Joe not to work too hard.

Jackie asked me to help her.

You can also say: Paul said not to worry about him.

Exercise 48.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. said (example) — said goodbye to me.
  2. Tell — Tell us (requires personal object).
  3. Say — Say something (no person).
  4. said — She said she would be here (no person).
  5. told — told me that (with personal object).
  6. said — said that (no personal object).
  7. told — told me to ask.
  8. said — said to ask (no person).
  9. tell … said — tell anybody what I said.
  10. tell … say — tell you … say anything to me.

  5. asked / told me to open my bag
  6. told him to mind his own business
