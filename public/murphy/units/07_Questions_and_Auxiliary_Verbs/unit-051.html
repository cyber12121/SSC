Unit 51 • Questions and auxiliary verbs

Auxiliary verbs (have/do/can etc.) I think so / I hope so etc.Short answers, echo questions, So/Neither, and responses with so/not

Master auxiliary verbs to streamline communication: echo questions, agreement with So/Neither, and expressions like I think so.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use auxiliary verbs (have, do, can, will) to prevent unnecessary repetition.
  • Form echo questions (Have you? Did it?) to show active listening.
  • Express agreement using So and Neither / Nor with inverted syntax.
  • Use formulaic responses with so and not (I think so, I hope not, I'm afraid so).

Part A — Theory

A. Avoiding Repetition with Auxiliary Verbs

You can use an auxiliary verb (have, do, can, was, will etc.) instead of repeating a whole verb phrase:

  • ‘Have you locked the door?’ ‘Yes, I have.’ (= I have locked the door)
  • Gary wasn't working, but Laura was. (= Laura was working)
  • Jessica could lend me the money, but she won't.

Use do / does / did for the present and past simple:

  • ‘Do you like onions?’ ‘Yes, I do.’
  • ‘Does Simon live in London?’ ‘He did, but he doesn't any more.’

To deny or contradict what somebody says:
• ‘You're sitting in my place.’ ‘No, I'm not.’
• ‘You didn't lock the door.’ ‘Yes, I did.’

B. Echo Questions • So / Neither / Nor

1. Echo Questions to show interest or surprise:

  • ‘I've just seen Steven.’ ‘Oh, have you? How is he?’
  • ‘Lisa isn't very well today.’ ‘Isn't she? What's wrong?’
  • ‘It rained every day on holiday.’ ‘Did it? What a shame!’

2. Agreement with So and Neither / Nor (with inversion):

Positive: So + auxiliary + subject

‘I'm tired.’ ‘So am I.’

‘I passed the exam.’ ‘So did Paul.’

Negative: Neither / Nor + aux + subject

‘I never read newspapers.’ ‘Neither do I.’

Sarah can't drive and neither can Mark.

Also: ‘Nor do I’ or ‘I don't either’.

C. I think so / I hope so / I'm afraid so

To avoid repeating a clause, we use so with certain verbs:

  • ‘Are those people Korean?’ ‘I think so.’ (= I think they are Korean)
  • ‘Is Kate working tomorrow?’ ‘I suppose so.’
  • ‘Will you be at home tonight?’ ‘I expect so.’
  • ‘Do you think it will rain?’ ‘I hope so.’
Negative Forms Note the standard negative patterns:
  • I don't think so • I don't expect so
  • I hope not • I'm afraid not (NOT I don't hope so)
  • I suppose not (or I don't suppose so)

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 51.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. were (example)
  2. doesn't — Ann doesn't like hot weather.
  3. was — He was here five minutes ago.
  4. has — Gary has travelled much.
  5. will — I don't think she will come.
  6. should — Do you think I should apply?
  7. won't — promise: I won't tell anybody.
  8. do — contradicting: Yes, I do!
  9. didn't — past simple negative: I didn't work.
  10. might — possibility: It might rain.
  11. am … isn't (or 'm not … is / can't … can)
  12. would … could … can't — I would if I could, but I can't.
Exercise 51.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. Are you? I'm not. (example)
  2. Don't you? I do. (example)
  3. Do you? I don't.
  4. Didn't you? I did.
  5. Aren't you? I am.
  6. Did you? I didn't.
Exercise 51.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. Neither am I. (example)
  2. Do you? What do you do? (example)
  3. So did I. (or: Did you? What did you watch?)
  4. Neither will I. (or: Won't you? Where will you be?)
  5. So do I. (or: Do you? What sort of books do you like?)
  6. So would I. (or: Would you? Where would you like to live?)
  7. Neither can I. (or: Can't you? Why not?)
  8. So am I. (or: Are you? Are you doing something nice?)
Exercise 51.4 — Key & Explanations
  1. I hope not. (example)
  2. I hope so.
  3. I hope not.
  4. I don't think so. — negative of think.
  5. I suppose so.
  6. I'm afraid so. — polite regretful affirmative.
  7. I think so.
  8. I'm afraid not. — polite regretful negative.
