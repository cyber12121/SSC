Unit 49 • Questions and auxiliary verbs

Questions 1Basic word order, do/does/did, who/what questions, and prepositions at the end

Master question structures: inversion with auxiliary verbs, subject vs. object questions, and preposition placement.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the auxiliary + subject question word order across all tenses.
  • Distinguish between subject questions (without do/does/did) and object questions (with do/does/did).
  • Place prepositions naturally at the end of wh-questions.
  • Form negative questions to express surprise or seek confirmation.

Part A — Theory

A. Basic Question Word Order

In questions, the subject is usually after the first auxiliary verb:

Statement → Question
Tom will be here.→Will Tom be here tomorrow?
You have worked.→Have you been working hard?
The house was built.→When was the house built?

Remember: the subject is after the first verb: Is Katherine working today? (NOT Is working Katherine)

B. Questions with do / does / did • Subject vs. Object Questions

In present simple questions we use do / does; in past simple questions we use did:

  • Do you live near here? • What time does the film start?
  • Did you sell your car? • Why did the train stop?
Object Question (use do/does/did)

Emma phoned somebody (object).

→ Who did Emma phone?

Diane said something.

→ What did Diane say?

Subject Question (NO do/does/did)

Somebody (subject) phoned Emma.

→ Who phoned Emma? (NOT Who did phone)

Something happened.

→ What happened? (NOT What did happen)

More Subject Question Examples
  • Who wants something to eat? (NOT Who does want)
  • How many people came to the party? (NOT did come)
  • Which bus goes to the centre? (NOT does go)

C. Prepositions at the End • Negative Questions

In questions beginning who / what / which / where, prepositions usually go at the end:

  • Where are you from? • What was the weather like?
  • Who do you want to speak to? • Which job has Tina applied for?

Formal style: To whom do you wish to speak?

Negative questions (isn't it? / didn't you? / don't you?):

  • To show surprise: Didn't you hear the doorbell? I rang it three times.
  • Expecting agreement: Haven't we met before? – Yes, I think we have.
  • With Why: Why don't we eat out tonight? (NOT Why we don't eat)

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 49.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. Where do you live? (example)
  2. Were you born there? — past simple of be.
  3. Are you married? — present simple of be.
  4. How long have you been married? — present perfect continuous duration.
  5. What do you do? — asking about occupation.
  6. What does your wife do? — third-person singular auxiliary does.
  7. Do you have (any) children? / Have you got (any) children?
  8. How old are they?
Exercise 49.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. Who hit you? (example — subject question)
  2. Who did you hit? (example — object question)
  3. Who paid the bill? / Who paid it? — subject question (no did).
  4. What are you worried about? — preposition at the end.
  5. What happened? — subject question (no did).
  6. What did she / Diane say? — object question.
  7. Who does it / this book belong to? — preposition at the end.
  8. Who lives in that house? / Who lives there? — subject question.
  9. What did you fall over? — object question with preposition.
  10. What fell off the shelf? — subject question.
  11. What does it / this word mean? — object question.
  12. Who was she / Sarah with? — preposition at the end.
  13. What are you looking for? — preposition at the end.
  14. Who does she / Emma remind you of? — preposition at the end.
