Unit 50 • Questions and auxiliary verbs

Questions 2 (do you know where …? / he asked me where …)Indirect questions, statement word order, if/whether, and reported questions

Master indirect questions (Do you know where...?) and reported questions with statement word order.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Construct indirect questions with standard statement word order (subject + verb).
  • Eliminate auxiliary do/does/did in indirect clauses.
  • Use if or whether for yes/no indirect and reported questions.
  • Convert direct interview questions into reported speech.

Part A — Theory

A. Indirect Questions (Do you know where … ? / Can you tell me … ?)

Compare direct and indirect questions:

Direct Question (Inverted)

Where has Tom gone?

What time is it?

Who are those people?

Where can I find Louise?

How much will it cost?

Indirect Question (Statement Order)

Do you know where Tom has gone? (NOT has Tom gone)

Do you know what time it is? (NOT is it)

I don't know who those people are.

Can you tell me where I can find Louise?

Do you have any idea how much it will cost?

Be Careful with do / does / did In indirect questions, do NOT use do / does / did:
  • What time does the film start? → Do you know what time the film starts? (NOT does the film start)
  • What do you mean? → Please explain what you mean.
  • Why did she leave early? → I wonder why she left early.

Use if or whether when there is no question word (yes/no questions):
Did anybody see you? → I don't know if (or whether) anybody saw me.

B. Reported Questions (He asked me where …)

The same statement word order applies to questions in reported speech. The verb also backshifts into the past:

Direct Question

‘Where are you going?’

‘What time do the shops close?’

‘Are you willing to travel?’

‘Why did you apply for the job?’

Reported Question

The officer asked us where we were going.

Clare wanted to know what time the shops closed.

She asked if / whether I was willing to travel.

She asked why I had applied for the job.

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 50.1 8 items • Choose the correct alternative

Which is right? Select (a), (b), or (c).

  1. (a) Do you know what time the film starts?
    (b) Do you know what time does the film start?
    (c) Do you know what time starts the film?
    (a is correct)
  2. (a) Why Amy does get up so early every day?
    (b) Why Amy gets up so early every day?
    (c) Why does Amy get up so early every day?
  3. (a) I want to know what this word means.
    (b) I want to know what does this word mean.
    (c) I want to know what means this word.
  4. (a) I can't remember where did I park the car.
    (b) I can't remember where I parked the car.
    (c) I can't remember where I did park the car.
  5. (a) Why you didn't phone me yesterday?
    (b) Why didn't you phone me yesterday?
    (c) Why you not phoned me yesterday?
  6. (a) Do you know where does Helen work?
    (b) Do you know where Helen does work?
    (c) Do you know where Helen works?
  7. (a) How much it costs to park here?
    (b) How much does it cost to park here?
    (c) How much it does cost to park here?
  8. (a) Tell me what you want.
    (b) Tell me what you do want.
    (c) Tell me what do you want.

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 50.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. a — Do you know what time the film starts? (statement word order)
  2. c — Why does Amy get up so early? (direct question)
  3. a — I want to know what this word means. (indirect)
  4. b — where I parked the car. (indirect)
  5. b — Why didn't you phone me? (direct negative question)
  6. c — Do you know where Helen works? (indirect)
  7. b — How much does it cost? (direct)
  8. a — Tell me what you want. (indirect imperative)
Exercise 50.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. Do you know what time it is? (example)
  2. How far is it to the airport? — direct question.
  3. I wonder how old Tom is. — indirect question statement order.
  4. How long have they been married? — direct question.
  5. Do you know how long they have been married? — indirect question.
  6. Could you tell me where the station is? — indirect polite request.
  7. I don't know whether (or if) anyone was injured in the accident.
  8. Do you know what time you will (or you'll) arrive tomorrow?
Exercise 50.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. He asked me where I was from. (example)
  2. She asked me how long I'd been / had been in London.
  3. They asked me if / whether I'd been / had been to London before.
  4. She asked me if / whether I liked London.
  5. He asked me where I was staying.
  6. She asked me how long I was going to stay.
  7. She asked me if / whether I thought London was / is expensive.
  8. They asked me why I'd come / had come / came to London.
