Unit 52 • Questions and auxiliary verbs

Question tags (do you? isn't it? etc.)Tag formation, intonation, polite requests, and special forms (aren't I?, shall we?)

Master question tags: positive/negative combinations, intonation nuances, special irregular forms, and polite request patterns.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Form negative tags for positive statements and positive tags for negative statements.
  • Distinguish between agreement-seeking tags (falling intonation) and real questions (rising intonation).
  • Form polite requests and inquiries with negative sentences and positive tags.
  • Apply irregular tag forms: aren't I?, shall we?, and will you?.

Part A — Theory

A. Question Tag Rules

Question tags are mini-questions added to the end of a sentence. We use an auxiliary verb (have, was, will, do etc.) + pronoun:

Positive sentence → Negative tag

Kate will be here soon, won't she?

There was a lot of traffic, wasn't there?

Joe should pass the exam, shouldn't he?

Karen plays the piano, doesn't she?

Negative sentence → Positive tag

Kate won't be late, will she?

They don't like us, do they?

You haven't eaten yet, have you?

You didn't lock the door, did you?

Answering negative questions: ‘You're not going out, are you?’ → ‘Yes.’ (= I am going out) • ‘No.’ (= I am not going out).

B. Intonation and Meaning

The meaning of a question tag depends on your voice tone:

  • Falling voice (↘): Not a real question – expecting agreement:
    ‘It's a nice day, isn't it?’ ‘Yes, beautiful.’
    ‘Paul doesn't look well today, does he?’ ‘No, he looks very tired.’
  • Rising voice (↗): A genuine question – you want information:
    ‘You haven't seen Kate today, have you?’ ‘No, I haven't.’
Polite Requests with Tags You can use a negative sentence + positive tag with rising voice to ask for things, favours, or information politely:
• You couldn't do me a favour, could you? – It depends what it is.
• You don't know where Karen is, do you? – Sorry, I have no idea.

C. Special Forms

Prompt Question Tag Example
Let's …shall we? (↗)Let's go for a walk, shall we?
Imperative / Don't …will you? (↘)Don't be late, will you?
I'm …aren't I? (= am I not?)I'm right, aren't I? – Yes, you are.

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 52.1 18 items • Complete with question tag

Complete these sentences with an appropriate question tag.

  1. Kate won't be late, ? – No, she's never late.
  2. You're tired, ? – Yes, a little.
  3. You travel a lot, ? – Yes, I love travelling.
  4. You weren't listening, ? – Yes, I was!
  5. Sarah doesn't know Ann, ? – No, they've never met.
  6. Jack's on holiday, ? – Yes, he's in Australia.
  7. It didn't take long to get here, ? – No, just ten minutes.
  8. You can speak German, ? – Yes, but not fluently.
  9. They won't mind if I take a picture, ? – No, of course they won't.
  10. There are a lot of people here, ? – Yes, more than I expected.
  11. Let's go and have coffee, ? – Yes, let's do that.
  12. This isn't very interesting, ? – No, not really.
  13. I'm too impatient, ? – Yes, you are sometimes.
  14. You wouldn't tell anyone, ? – No, of course not.
  15. Helen has lived here a long time, ? – Yes, 20 years.
  16. I shouldn't have lost my temper, ? – No, but that's all right.
  17. He'd never met her before, ? – No, that was the first time.
  18. Don't forget to call me, ? – No, I won't forget.

In these situations you expect your friend to agree with you. Write a sentence with a question tag.

  1. You look out of the window. The sky is blue and the sun is shining. (beautiful day)
    You say:
  2. You're with a friend outside a restaurant looking at very high prices. (expensive)
    You say: It
  3. You and a colleague really enjoyed a finished training course. (great)
    You say: The course
  4. Your friend's hair is much shorter than when you last met. (have / your hair / cut)
    You say: You
  5. You're listening to a woman singing and like her voice very much. (a good voice)
    You say: She
  6. You're trying on a jacket in a shop and you don't like what you see. (not / look / right)
    You say: It
  7. You and a friend are walking over an old wooden bridge with broken parts. (not / very safe)
    You say: This bridge
Exercise 52.3 6 items • Polite requests with negative sentence + positive tag

In these situations you are asking for information or asking people to do things politely using negative sentence + positive tag.

  1. You need a pen. Perhaps Jane has one. Ask her.
  2. You have to move a heavy table. You want Joe to help you. Ask him.
    Joe, you
  3. You're looking for Sarah. Perhaps Lisa knows where she is. Ask her.
    Lisa, you
  4. You want to borrow a tennis racket. Perhaps Helen has one. Ask her.
    Helen,
  5. Anna has a car and you need a lift to the station. Perhaps she'll take you. Ask her.
    Anna,
  6. You're looking for your keys. Perhaps Robert has seen them. Ask him.
    Robert,

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 52.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. will she (example)
  2. aren't you (example)
  3. don't you — present simple positive → negative tag.
  4. were you — past continuous negative → positive tag.
  5. does she — present simple negative → positive tag.
  6. isn't he — Jack is → isn't he.
  7. did it — past simple negative → positive tag.
  8. can't you — modal positive → negative tag.
  9. will they — future negative → positive tag.
  10. aren't there — There are → aren't there.
  11. shall we — after Let's.
  12. is it — This isn't → is it.
  13. aren't I — after I'm.
  14. would you — wouldn't → would you.
  15. hasn't she — present perfect positive → negative tag.
  16. should I — modal negative → positive tag.
  17. had he — past perfect negative (never) → positive tag.
  18. will you — after negative imperative Don't.
Exercise 52.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. It's a beautiful day, isn't it? (example)
  2. It's (very) expensive, isn't it?
  3. The course was great, wasn't it?
  4. You've had your hair cut, haven't you? (or You had your hair cut, didn't you?)
  5. She has a good voice, doesn't she? (or hasn't she? / She's got ... hasn't she?)
  6. It doesn't look right, does it?
  7. This bridge isn't very safe, is it? (or doesn't look very safe, does it?)
Exercise 52.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. Jane, you don't have a pen I could borrow, do you? (example)
  2. Joe, you couldn't help me (with this table), could you?
  3. Lisa, you don't know where Sarah is, do you? (or you haven't seen Sarah, have you?)
  4. Helen, you don't have a tennis racket, do you? (or you haven't got a tennis racket, have you?)
  5. Anna, you couldn't take me to the station, could you? (or couldn't give me a lift...)
  6. Robert, you haven't seen my keys, have you?
