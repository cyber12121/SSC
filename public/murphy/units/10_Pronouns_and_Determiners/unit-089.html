Unit 89 • Pronouns and determiners

both / both of, neither / neither of, either / either ofquantifiers for two items: both (of), neither (of), either (of), both … and, neither … nor, either … or

We use both, neither, and either to talk about two things or people. We pair them with 'of' before determiners and pronouns, and use paired conjunctions.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use both, neither, either with nouns or independently for exactly two people or things.
  • Use both of / neither of / either of with determiners and pronouns (both of us, neither of them).
  • Construct paired correlative clauses: both … and …, neither … nor …, either … or ….
  • Differentiate two-item quantifiers (both, neither, either) from multiple-item quantifiers (all, none, any).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Both, Neither, Either (Two Things)

We use both / neither / either for two things. You can use them with a noun:

Both restaurants are good. (not the both restaurants)
Neither restaurant is expensive.
We can go to either restaurant. I don't mind. (= one or the other)
I haven't been to either restaurant before. (= not one or the other)

You can also use them without a noun:

‘Which do you prefer, basketball or tennis?’ ‘I like both.’
‘Is your friend British or American?’ ‘Neither. She's Australian.’
‘Do you want tea or coffee?’ ‘Either. I don't mind.’

Part B: Both of … / Neither of … / Either of …

We use both of / neither of / either of + the / these / my / Tom's etc.:

Both of these restaurants are good.  or  Both these restaurants …
Neither of the restaurants we went to was expensive.
I haven't been to either of those restaurants.

With pronouns (us / you / them), we must use of:

Can either of you speak Russian?
I asked two people, but neither of them knew.
Both of us were tired. (not Both us)

After neither of … the verb can be singular or plural:

Neither of them is at home.  or  Neither of them are at home.

Part C: Both … and • Neither … nor • Either … or

Both Chris and Paul were late.
I was both tired and hungry when I arrived home.
Neither Chris nor Paul came to the party.
We neither saw nor heard anything.
She's either Spanish or Italian.
Either you apologise, or I'll never speak to you again.

Part D: Two Things vs More Than Two

Compare either / neither / both (two items) with any / none / all (more than two):

Two Things

There are two good hotels. You could stay at either of them.

We tried two hotels:
• Neither of them had a room.
• Both of them were full.

More Than Two

There are many good hotels. You could stay at any of them.

We tried a lot of hotels:
• None of them had a room.
• All of them were full.
