Unit 91 • Pronouns and determiners

each and everyeach (individual, one by one) vs every (group, all together); each of; everyone vs every one

We use 'each' when thinking of items separately one by one (usual for small numbers or two items). We use 'every' when thinking of items as a whole group (large numbers or recurring events).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish between each (individual, separate items, one by one) and every (collective, all together as a group).
  • Apply each to two items or small numbers, and every to large numbers or frequency intervals.
  • Use each of + plural determiner/pronoun vs every one of.
  • Position each in mid-sentence or after amounts: ten pounds each.
  • Differentiate everyone (people only = everybody) from every one (two words, people or things).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Each vs Every (Individual vs Group)

Each and every are often similar in meaning:

Each time I see you, you look different.  or  Every time I see you …

However, they have important differences:

Each (one by one, separately)

We use each when we think of things separately, one by one:
• Study each sentence carefully. (= study them one by one)
• More usual for a small number:
There were four books. Each book was a different colour.
• Can be used for two things:
In football, each team has eleven players. (not every team)

Every (as a group / all)

We use every when we think of things as a group (similar to all):
• Every window in the house was open. (= all the windows)
• More usual for a large number:
Kate has read every book in the library.
I'd like to visit every country in the world.
• Used to say how often something happens:
How often do you use your car? Every day. (not Each day)
There's a bus every ten minutes.

Part B: Structural Patterns with Each and Every

We use each with or without a noun:

None of the rooms are the same. Each room is different.  or  Each is different.  or  Each one is different.
Each of the books was a different colour. (not each of books)
Each of them was a different colour.
Read each of these sentences carefully.

We use every with a noun:

She's read every book in the library.
We do not use every alone, but we say every one:
A: Have you read all these books?
B: Yes, every one.
We say every one of … (not every of):
I've read every one of those books. / I've read every one of them.

Part C: Each in Mid-Sentence and After Amounts

We also use each in the middle of a sentence:

The students were each given a book. (= Each student was given a book.)
Sonia and I each had ten pounds.

We say a dollar each, 40 pence each:

These oranges are 40 pence each. (each = for one orange)
Those postcards cost a pound each.

Part D: Everyone (1 Word) vs Every One (2 Words)

Everyone (one word = everybody)

Used only for people:
Everyone enjoyed the party. (= Everybody enjoyed the party.)
Amy is popular. Everyone likes her.

Every One (two words = each one)

Used for things or people:
Sarah goes to lots of parties and she goes to every one. (= every party)
I dropped a tray of glasses. Unfortunately every one broke.
