Unit 86 • Pronouns and determiners

no / none / any, nothing / nobody etc.negative words: no + noun, none alone/of, nothing, nobody, nowhere vs not … any

We use 'no + noun', 'none' without a noun, and negative pronouns (nothing/nobody/nowhere) with affirmative verbs. Double negatives are avoided in standard English.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the difference between no + noun, none (without a noun), and none of.
  • Use nothing, nobody, no-one, nowhere with affirmative verbs to express negative statements.
  • Avoid double negatives: say I said nothing (not I didn't say nothing).
  • Convert sentences between negative words (nothing) and not … any.
  • Contrast nobody/nothing (negative) with anybody/anything (it doesn't matter who/what).

Part A — Theory

Part A: No + Noun vs None (Without Noun)

We use no + noun (no bus, no shops etc.). No = not a or not any:

We had to walk home. There was no bus. (= There wasn't a bus.)
Sarah will have no trouble finding a job. (= won't have any trouble)
There were no shops open. (= There weren't any shops open.)
No reason was given for the change of plan. (at the start of a sentence)

We use none without a noun:

‘How much money do you have?’ ‘None.’ (= no money)
All the tickets have been sold. There are none left. (= no tickets left)

We use none of … before determiners and pronouns:

This money is all yours. None of it is mine.
None of the students were / was happy. (verb can be singular or plural)

I have no luggage.
There are no buses on Sunday.

‘How much luggage do you have?’ ‘None.’ or ‘I don't have any.’
There aren't any.

Part B: Nothing, Nobody / No-one, Nowhere

You can use these words at the beginning of a sentence or alone as answers to questions:

‘What's going to happen?’ ‘Nobody knows. / No-one knows.’
‘What happened?’ ‘Nothing.’
‘Where are you going?’ ‘Nowhere. I'm staying here.’

You can also use these words after a verb, especially after be and have:

The house is empty. There's nobody living there.
We had nothing to eat.

Nothing / nobody etc. = not + anything / anybody etc.:

I said nothing.  =  I didn't say anything.
Jane told nobody about her plans.  =  Jane didn't tell anybody.
They have nowhere to live.  =  They don't have anywhere to live.

Crucial Rule – No Double Negatives: With nothing/nobody etc., do not use a negative verb:

I said nothing. (not I didn't say nothing)
Nobody came. (not Nobody didn't come)

Part C: Pronouns with Nobody / No-one

After nobody / no-one, you can use they / them / their:

Nobody is perfect, are they? (= is he or she perfect?)
No-one did what I asked them to do. (= him or her)
Nobody in the class did their homework. (= his or her homework)

Part D: No- vs Any- (Contrast in Meaning)

Compare negative words (no / nobody / nothing) and free-choice words (any / anybody / anything):

no / nothing / nobody (negative)

There was no bus, so we walked home.
‘What do you want to eat?’ ‘Nothing. I'm not hungry.’
It's a difficult job. Nobody wants to do it.

any / anything / anybody (any = no matter which)

You can take any bus. They all go to the centre.
I'm so hungry. I could eat anything.
It's a very easy job. Anybody can do it.
