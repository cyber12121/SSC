Unit 82 • Pronouns and determiners

myself / yourself / themselves etc.reflexive pronouns (myself, yourself etc.) and each other

We use reflexive pronouns (myself, yourself, themselves etc.) when the subject and object are the same person or thing, or to emphasize who performed an action.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the singular (myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself) and plural (ourselves, yourselves, themselves) reflexive pronouns.
  • Understand when subject and object are the same: Steve introduced himself.
  • Recognize verbs that normally do not use reflexive pronouns: feel, relax, concentrate, meet, wash, shave, dress.
  • Distinguish between reflexive pronouns (-selves) and reciprocal pronouns (each other / one another).
  • Use reflexive pronouns for emphasis: I made it myself (= me, not somebody else).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Reflexive Pronouns (Subject & Object Same)

Study this example:

Steve introduced himself to the other guests.
(Subject: Steve • Object: himself → Steve and himself are the same person)

We use myself / yourself / himself etc. (reflexive pronouns) when the subject and object are the same:

Singular (-self)

myself
yourself (one person)
himself / herself / itself

Plural (-selves)

ourselves
yourselves (more than one person)
themselves
I don't want you to pay for me. I'll pay for myself. (not pay for me)
Amy had a great holiday. She really enjoyed herself.
Do you talk to yourself sometimes? (said to one person)
If you want more to eat, help yourselves. (said to more than one person)
Object Pronoun (Different person)

Lisa introduced me to the other guests.
(Lisa = subject, me = different object)

Reflexive Pronoun (Same person)

I introduced myself to the other guests.
(I = subject, myself = same object)

Part B: Verbs Not Normally Used with Reflexives

We do not use myself etc. after feel, relax, concentrate, meet:

I feel nervous. I can't relax.
You need to concentrate. (not concentrate yourself)
What time shall we meet tomorrow? (not meet ourselves)

Normally we do not use myself etc. after wash, shave, dress:

He got up, washed, shaved and dressed. (not washed himself etc.)
You can also say get dressed: He got dressed.

Part C: -selves vs Each Other (One Another)

Compare themselves and each other:

Themselves (Reflexive)

Kate and Joe stood in front of the mirror and looked at themselves.
(= Kate and Joe looked at Kate and Joe in the mirror)

Each Other (Reciprocal)

Kate looked at Joe, and Joe looked at Kate. They looked at each other.
(= Kate looked at Joe, and Joe looked at Kate)

You can also use one another instead of each other:

How long have you and Ben known each other?  or  … known one another?
Sue and Alice don't like each other.  or  … don't like one another.
Do they live near each other?  or  … near one another?

Part D: Emphatic Use (Myself = Me, Not Somebody Else)

We also use myself / yourself etc. for emphasis (to say 'that person, not another person'):

‘Who repaired your bike?’ ‘I repaired it myself.’
(= I repaired it, not somebody else. 'Myself' emphasizes 'I'.)
I'm not going to do your work for you. You can do it yourself. (= you, not me)
Let's paint the house ourselves. It will be much cheaper.
The film itself wasn't very good, but I loved the music.
Lisa doesn't think so herself.  or  Lisa herself doesn't think so.
