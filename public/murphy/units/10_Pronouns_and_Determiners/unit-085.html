Unit 85 • Pronouns and determiners

some and anysome/any, somebody/anybody, something/anything, somewhere/anywhere

In general, we use 'some' in positive sentences and 'any' in negative sentences and most questions. 'Any' also means 'it doesn't matter which'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use some in positive statements and any in negative statements.
  • Distinguish question contexts: some when offering, requesting, or expecting a 'yes', vs any for open inquiry.
  • Recognize negative meanings created by without, hardly, refuse + any.
  • Apply any with the meaning 'it doesn't matter which/who/what/where'.
  • Treat somebody / anyone as singular grammatically, but refer back with they / them / their.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Positive (Some) vs Negative (Any)

In general, we use some (also somebody / someone / something / somewhere) in positive sentences, and any (also anybody / anyone / anything / anywhere) in negative sentences:

some (positive)

We bought some flowers.
He's busy. He has some work to do.
There's somebody at the door.
I want something to eat.

any (negative)

We didn't buy any flowers.
He's lazy. He never does any work.
There isn't anybody at the door.
I don't want anything to eat.

We use any in sentences with a negative meaning:

She went out without any money. (= she didn't take any money)
He refused to eat anything. (= he didn't eat anything)
It's a very easy exam. Hardly anybody fails. (= almost nobody fails)

Part B: Questions with Some vs Any

We use both some and any in questions:

When to use some: We use some / somebody / something when we expect the answer to be 'yes', or when we offer or ask for things:
• Are you waiting for somebody? (I think you are waiting for somebody)
• Can I have some sugar, please? (asking for something that exists)
• Would you like something to eat? (offering something)
When to use any: In most other questions, we use any because we do not know if the person or thing exists:
• Do you have any luggage? (maybe you do, maybe not)
• Is there anybody in the house? (maybe there is, maybe not)

Part C: If + Any

We often use any after if:

Let me know if you need anything.
If anyone has any questions, I'll be pleased to answer them.

The following sentences also carry the conditional idea of if:

I'm sorry for any trouble I've caused. (= if I have caused any trouble)
The police want to speak to anyone who saw the accident. (= if there is anyone)

Part D: Any = 'It Doesn't Matter Which'

We also use any with the meaning ‘it doesn't matter which / what / who / where’:

You can take any bus. They all go to the centre. (= it doesn't matter which bus you take)
Come and see me any time you want.
We forgot to lock the door. Anybody could have come in.

A: I'm hungry. I want something to eat.
B: What would you like?
A: I don't mind. Anything. (= it doesn't matter what)

A: Let's go out somewhere.
B: Where shall we go?
A: Anywhere. I just want to go out.

Part E: Pronoun Agreement (They/Them/Their)

Somebody / someone / anybody / anyone take a singular verb, but we refer back to them using plural pronouns (they / them / their):

Someone is here to see you. (singular verb)
Someone has forgotten their umbrella. (= his or her umbrella)
If anybody wants to leave early, they can. (= he or she can)
