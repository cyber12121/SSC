Unit 83 • Pronouns and determiners

a friend of mine / my own house / on my own / by myselfpossessive pronouns with of (a friend of mine) & own & on my own / by myself

We use 'a friend of mine' to talk about one of our friends, 'my own' to emphasize exclusive ownership or personal action, and 'on my own' or 'by myself' to mean alone.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the double possessive construction: a friend of mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs and a friend of Tom's.
  • Use own with possessive determiners (my own room, her own business) to mean exclusive or unshared.
  • Use own for doing something yourself: He cuts his own hair / grow our own vegetables.
  • Express 'alone' with both on my own and by myself.

Part A — Theory

Part A: A Friend of Mine / A Friend of Tom's

We say (a friend) of mine / yours / his / hers / ours / theirs (= one of my/your etc. friends):

I'm going to a wedding on Saturday. A friend of mine is getting married. (not a friend of me)
We went on holiday with some friends of ours. (not some friends of us)
Harry had an argument with a neighbour of his.
It was a good idea of yours to go to the cinema.

In the same way, we say (a friend) of my sister's / (a friend) of Tom's etc.:

That woman over there is a friend of my sister's. (= one of my sister's friends)
It was a good idea of Tom's to go to the cinema.

Part B: My Own … / Your Own …

We say my own / your own / her own etc. (not an own house, an own car):

my own house • your own car • her own room

My own … = something that is only mine, not shared or borrowed:

I don't want to share a room with anybody. I want my own room.
Vicky and Gary would like to have their own house.
It's a shame that the apartment hasn't got its own parking space.
It's my own fault that I have no money. I buy too many things I don't need.
Why don't you use your own? (= your own car)

You can also say a room of my own / a house of your own / problems of his own:

I'd like to have a room of my own.
He has too many problems of his own to help you.

Part C: Doing Something Yourself (Cut His Own Hair)

We also use own to say that we do something ourselves instead of somebody else doing it for us:

Paul usually cuts his own hair. (= he cuts it himself)
I'd like to have a garden so that I could grow my own vegetables. (= grow them myself instead of buying them from shops)

Part D: On My Own / By Myself (= Alone)

On my own and by myself both mean 'alone'. You can use either form:

on + possessive + own

on my / your own
on his / her / its own
on our / your / their own

by + reflexive

by myself / yourself
by himself / herself / itself
by ourselves / yourselves / themselves
I like living on my own.  or  I like living by myself.
Some people prefer to live on their own.  or  … live by themselves.
Jack was sitting on his own in a corner.  or  … sitting by himself …
Did you go on holiday on your own?  or  … by yourself?
