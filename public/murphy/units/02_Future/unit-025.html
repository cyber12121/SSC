Unit 25 • Tenses Basics

when I do and when I've done · if and whenTime and condition clauses

Master the rule for future time clauses: use present tenses (never will) with when, as soon as, before, after, until, and learn when vs if.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use the present simple (not will) in time clauses with when, before, after, as soon as, until.
  • Use the present perfect (when I've done) for an action completed before the main clause begins.
  • Differentiate between when (certain to happen) and if (possible but not certain).

Part A — Theory

A. Time clauses with when, before, after, as soon as, until

A: What time will you phone me tomorrow?
B: I'll phone you when I get home from work.

The sentence has two parts: the main clause (I'll phone you) and the time clause (when I get home). The time is future, but we use a present tense (not will) in the time clause:

  • We'll go out when it stops raining. (not when it will stop)
  • Wait here until I come back. (not until I will come)
  • I want to see Sophie before she goes away next week.
  • As soon as we get any more information, we'll let you know.

B. when I've done / after you've had

You can also use the present perfect (have done) after when / after / until / as soon as to show that one thing is completely finished before the other starts:

  • Can I borrow that book when you've finished it?
  • Don't say anything until you've heard the whole story.

Do not use the present perfect if the two things happen together (use present simple): When I phone Kate, I'll ask her about the party.

C. when and if

when (certain)

We use when for things that are sure to happen:

• I'm going out later. When I go out, I'll get some bread.

if (possible)

We use if for things that will possibly happen:

• I might go out later. If I go out, I'll get some bread.
