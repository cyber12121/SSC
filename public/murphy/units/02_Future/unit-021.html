Unit 21 • Tenses Basics

will and shall 1Instant decisions, offers, promises, shall

Learn how to use I'll when making spontaneous decisions, offers, and promises, and how to use Shall I/we ... ? for suggestions.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use I'll (= I will) when deciding to do something at the moment of speaking.
  • Use I'll for offering, agreeing, promising, or asking somebody to do something.
  • Use won't when refusing to do something.
  • Use Shall I ... ? / Shall we ... ? to ask for advice or make offers and suggestions.

Part A — Theory

A. Decisions at the moment of speaking (I'll ...)

We use I'll ... (= I will) when we've just decided to do something. We announce our decision:

  • Oh, I left the door open. I'll go and shut it.
  • 'What would you like to drink?' 'I'll have orange juice, please.'
  • 'Did you call Max?' 'Oh no, I forgot. I'll call him now.'

We do not use the present simple (I do / I go) in these sentences: I'll phone him now (not I phone him now).

We often use I think I'll ... / I don't think I'll ...:

  • I'm a little hungry. I think I'll have something to eat.
  • I don't think I'll go out tonight. I'm too tired.

B. Offering, agreeing, promising, refusing

  • Offering: That bag looks heavy. I'll help you with it. (not I help)
  • Agreeing: Sure, I'll give it to him when I see him this afternoon.
  • Promising: Thanks for lending me the money. I'll pay you back on Friday.
  • Refusing (won't): I've tried to give her advice, but she won't listen. The car won't start.

C. Shall I ... ? / Shall we ... ?

Shall I ... ? / Shall we ... ? is used to ask somebody's opinion (especially in offers or suggestions):

  • Shall I open the window? (= do you want me to open the window?)
  • I've got no money. What shall I do? (= what do you suggest?)
  • Shall we go? (= are you ready?)
