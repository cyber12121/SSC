Unit 23 • Tenses Basics

I will and I'm going toComparing future intentions and predictions

Learn the essential distinction between deciding in the moment (will) versus prior intention and evidence (going to).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Contrast I will (decided at moment of speaking) with I am going to (decided before speaking).
  • Understand situations where going to is required due to present evidence.
  • Recognize when both will and going to are acceptable for predictions.

Part A — Theory

A. Future actions: I will vs I am going to

I will (decision now)

We use will when we decide to do something at the time of speaking:

• 'Did you phone Bob?' 'Oh no, I forgot. I'll phone him now.'

• That bag looks heavy. I'll help you with it.

I am going to (prior decision)

We use going to when we have already decided before speaking:

• 'Did you phone Bob?' 'Yes, but there was no answer. I'm going to phone him later.'

• I'm going to paint this room next weekend.

B. Future situations: Predictions

We can use both will and going to for general predictions:

  • I think the weather will be nice later. or ... is going to be nice later.

But when there is something in the present situation (evidence) showing what will happen, use going to:

• Look at those black clouds! It's going to rain. (not It will rain)

• I feel terrible. I think I'm going to be sick. (not I will be sick)

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 23.1 11 items • will ('ll) or (be) going to

Complete the sentences using will ('ll) or (be) going to.

  1. A: Why are you turning on the TV?
    B: the news. (I / watch)
  2. A: I forgot my wallet. I don't have any money.
    B: Not to worry. you some. (I / lend)
  3. A: Why are you filling that bucket with water?
    B: the car. (I / wash)
  4. A: I don't know how to use the washing machine.
    B: It's easy. you. (I / show)
  5. A: I've decided to paint this room.
    B: That's nice. What colour it? (you / paint)
  6. A: Where are you going? Are you going shopping?
    B: Yes, some things for dinner tonight. (I / buy)
  7. A: What would you like to eat?
    B: a pizza, please. (I / have)
  8. A: This food doesn't taste very good, does it?
    B: No, it's horrible. it. (I / not / finish)
  9. A: Tom is starting an evening class next month.
    B: Is he? What ? (he / study)
  10. A: Did you call Lisa?
    B: Oh, no. I completely forgot. her now. (I / call)
  11. A: Has Dan decided what to do when he leaves school?
    B: Yes. Everything is planned. First a holiday for a few weeks. (he / have)
    Then a management training course. (he / do)
Exercise 23.2 6 situations • will ('ll) or (be) going to

Read the situations and complete the sentences using will ('ll) or (be) going to.

  1. You want some coffee. You go to the kitchen to make some.
    You say: some coffee. Would you like some?
  2. You're speaking to a friend and arranging to meet. You suggest a time and place.
    You say: you at 10.30 in the hotel lobby, OK? (I / see)
  3. You have decided to sell your car. You tell a friend of yours.
    You say: I don't need my car any more. it. (I / sell)
  4. Your friend is worried because she has lost her driving licence.
    You say: Don't worry. I'm sure it. (you / find)
  5. (5a) You have an old camera that is broken. You have decided to throw it away.
    You say: This camera is broken. it away. (I / throw)
    (5b) Your friend loves old cameras and says: Don't throw it away! (I / have)
  6. (6a) Joe has to go to the airport tomorrow. Amy offers to take him.
    Amy says: Don't worry about getting to the airport, Joe. you. (I / take)
    (6b) Later, Paul offers to take Joe. Joe says: Thanks, Paul, but me. (Amy / take)

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 23

Category Rule Correct Example
Decision Timing will = decided now; going to = decided before I'll phone him now. I'm going to paint this room next week.
Present Evidence Requires going to when present signs indicate the outcome Look at that ceiling! It's going to fall down.
General Predictions Either will or going to is acceptable I think the weather will be / is going to be nice.
