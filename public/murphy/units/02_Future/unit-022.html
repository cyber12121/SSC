Unit 22 • Tenses Basics

will and shall 2Predictions, opinions, future facts

Learn how to use will to predict future events and express opinions, and distinguish it from arrangements.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Understand when to use will for predicting future facts and situations.
  • Never use will for arrangements already made (use present continuous or going to).
  • Use will with probably, I'm sure, I think, I wonder, and use the present simple after I hope.

Part A — Theory

A. We do not use will to say what somebody has already arranged or decided to do

  • Diane is working next Saturday. (not Diane will work)
  • Are you going to watch TV this evening? (not Will you watch)

For arrangements and decisions made before speaking, use present continuous (Unit 19) or going to (Unit 20).

B. We use will to talk about the future in general (predictions & facts)

• Kate is traveling in Europe. She loves traveling. She'll have a great time.

• Jill is 16 now. Next year she will be 17.

• When the new road is finished, my journey to work will be much quicker.

We often use will ('ll) with:

  • probably: I'll probably be home late this evening.
  • I'm sure: Don't worry about the exam. I'm sure you'll pass.
  • I think / I don't think: Do you think Sarah will like the present we bought her?
  • I wonder: I wonder what will happen.

After I hope, we generally use the present (will is also possible):

  • I hope Kate passes the driving test. (or I hope Kate will pass)
  • I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.

C. Generally we use will ('ll) with all persons

You can also use shall instead of will only with I and we:

  • We shall need / We will need money for the trip.
  • I shall be / I will be late this evening.

Do not use shall with he / she / it / they / you.

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 22

Category Rule Correct Example
Predicting Future Facts Things that are not arranged, but predicted or factual Jill is 16 now. Next year she will be 17.
Opinion Expressions I think / I don't think / I'm sure / probably + will I'm sure you'll pass. I don't think it will rain.
I hope + present Use present simple after 'I hope' I hope you have a nice trip (not hope you will have).
