Unit 113 • Conjunctions and prepositions

although / though / even though, in spite of / despiteconcession clauses & prepositions: although + clause vs in spite of / despite + noun/-ing

We use although, though, even though, in spite of and despite to express contrast. Although, though and even though are followed by a subject + verb, while in spite of and despite are followed by a noun, pronoun or -ing.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Express contrast using although followed by subject + verb: Although it rained a lot, they had a good time.
  • Distinguish between although (contrast/concession) and because (cause/reason).
  • Use in spite of and despite followed by a noun, pronoun, or -ing form: in spite of the rain / despite being very tired.
  • Avoid the common error: never say despite of.
  • Form complex clauses with in spite of the fact (that) ... and despite the fact (that) ....
  • Use though as an informal alternative to although, especially at the end of spoken sentences.
  • Use even though for stronger contrast (and distinguish it from even alone).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Although (Subject + Verb)

Study this example situation:

Last year Paul and Sarah had a holiday by the sea.
It rained a lot, but they had a good time.
You can say:
Although it rained a lot, they had a good time. (= It rained a lot, but they ...)
or
In spite of / Despite the rain, they had a good time.

After although we use a subject + verb:

• Although it rained a lot, they had a good time.
• I didn't apply for the job although I had the necessary qualifications.
Although (Contrast)

We went out although it was raining heavily.
(= We went out in spite of the bad weather)

Because (Reason)

We didn't go out because it was raining heavily.
(= The rain was the reason we stayed inside)

Part B: In Spite Of and Despite (+ Noun / Pronoun / -ing)

After in spite of or despite, we use a noun, a pronoun (this/that/what etc.) or -ing:

• In spite of the rain, we had a good time. (noun)
• She wasn't well, but in spite of this she continued working. (pronoun)
• In spite of what I said yesterday, I still love you. (pronoun clause)
• I didn't apply for the job in spite of having the necessary qualifications. (-ing)
Important Note on Despite:
Despite has the exact same meaning as in spite of, but does NOT take of:
She wasn't well, but despite this she continued working. (NOT despite of this)

You can also say in spite of the fact (that) … and despite the fact (that) … + subject + verb:

I didn't apply for the job in spite of the fact (that) I had the necessary qualifications.
or … despite the fact (that) I had the necessary qualifications.
In spite of / Despite (Contrast)

We went out in spite of the rain. (or despite the rain)

Because of (Reason)

We didn't go out because of the rain.

Part C: Comparing Although and In Spite Of / Despite

Although + Subject + Verb

Although the traffic was bad, we arrived on time.
(NOT in spite of the traffic was bad)

I couldn't sleep although I was very tired.

In Spite Of / Despite + Noun or -ing

In spite of the traffic, we arrived on time.
Despite the traffic, we arrived on time.

I couldn't sleep despite being very tired.
(NOT despite I was tired)

Part D: Though and Even Though

Though has the same meaning as although:

I didn't apply for the job though I had the necessary qualifications.

In spoken English, we often place though at the end of a sentence:

• The house isn't so nice. I like the garden though. (= but I like the garden)
• I see them every day. I've never spoken to them though. (= but I've never spoken to them)

Even though is stronger and more emphatic than although:

• Even though I was really tired, I couldn't sleep. (NOT even I was really tired)

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 113

Category Rule Correct Example
although / though / even thoughFollowed by subject + verb to express concession/contrastAlthough it was raining heavily, we went out.
in spite of / despiteFollowed by noun, pronoun (this/that/what), or -ing; no 'of' with despiteIn spite of the rain / Despite being very tired.
in spite of the fact (that)Allows a full clause with subject + verb after in spite of / despiteDespite the fact that I had the qualifications.
though at endUsed at the end of a spoken sentence to mean 'however' / 'but'The house isn't nice. I like the garden though.
even though vs evenEven though is an emphatic conjunction; even alone cannot connect clausesEven though I was tired, I couldn't sleep.
