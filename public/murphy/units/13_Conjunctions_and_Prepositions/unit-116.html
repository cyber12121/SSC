Unit 116 • Conjunctions and prepositions

as (as I walked … / as I was … etc.)simultaneous actions (at the same time as) and reasons (because / since)

We use as when two things happen simultaneously ('at the same time as') or to give a reason ('because / since'). For sequential actions, use when rather than as.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use as to connect two actions happening simultaneously: We waved to Liz as she drove away.
  • Combine as with past continuous for actions in progress: Kate slipped as she was getting off the bus.
  • Use just as for precise momentary timing: Just as I sat down, the doorbell rang.
  • Express gradual changes over time with as: As the day went on, the weather got worse.
  • Distinguish between as (simultaneous actions) and when (sequential actions or stages of life).
  • Use as (and since) to mean because: As I was hungry, I decided to find somewhere to eat.

Part A — Theory

Part A: As = At the Same Time As

You can use as when two things happen together at the same time:

• We all waved goodbye to Liz as she drove away. (we waved and she drove away at the same time)
• As I walked along the street, I looked in the shop windows.
• Can you turn off the light as you go out, please?

Or you can say that something happened as you were doing something else (in the middle of doing it):

• Kate slipped as she was getting off the bus.
• We met Paul as we were leaving the hotel.

You can also use just as (= exactly at that moment):

• Just as I sat down, the doorbell rang.
• I had to leave just as the conversation was getting interesting.

We also use as when two things develop or change together over time:

• As the day went on, the weather got worse.
• I began to enjoy the job more as I got used to it.

Part B: Compare As and When (Simultaneous vs Sequential)

as (at the same time)

We use as only if two things happen at the same time:
As I drove home, I listened to music. (= simultaneous)

when (one after another)

Use when (not as) if one event happens after another:
When I got home, I had something to eat. (NOT as I got home)

as (= because / reasons)

David wasn't in the office as he was on holiday. (= because he was on holiday)

As they lived near us, we used to see them quite often. (= because they lived near us)

when (periods of time / life)

David lost his passport when he was on holiday. (= during the time he was away)

When they lived near us, we used to see them quite often. (= at that period of time)

Part C: As = Because

As also means ‘because’:

• As I was hungry, I decided to find somewhere to eat. (= because I was hungry)
• As it's late and we're tired, let's get a taxi home. (= because it's late ...)
• We watched TV all evening as we didn't have anything better to do.
• As I don't watch TV any more, I gave my television to a friend of mine.

You can also use since in this way:

Since it's late and we're tired, let's get a taxi home.
