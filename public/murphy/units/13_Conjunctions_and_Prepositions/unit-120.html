Unit 120 • Conjunctions and prepositions

by and until, by the time …deadlines, durations & time sequences: by (= not later than), until (= up to), by the time + clause

We use by to indicate a deadline ('not later than'). We use until to state how long an ongoing state continues. We use by the time + clause to indicate an event that occurs before or at the moment of another.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use by + time to set a firm deadline (meaning not later than): They should arrive by Monday.
  • Use until (or till) to specify the duration of an ongoing state: Wait until it stops raining.
  • Contrast ongoing duration (until) with completion deadlines (by): I'll be working until 11.30 vs I'll have finished by 11.30.
  • Construct clauses with by the time in the future (+ present simple + future perfect): By the time we get to the cinema, the film will already have started.
  • Construct clauses with by the time in the past (+ past simple + past perfect): By the time she arrived, most of the other guests had left.
  • Use by then or by that time as short adverbial references.

Part A — Theory

Part A: By (= Not Later Than) and Until (= How Long)

by … = not later than:

• I sent the documents today, so they should arrive by Monday. (= on or before Monday, not later than Monday)
• We'd better hurry. We have to be home by 5 o'clock. (= at or before 5 o'clock)
• Where's Sarah? She should be here by now. (= now or before now – she should already be here)
• This milk has to be used by 14 August.

We use until (or till) to say how long a situation continues:

• ‘Shall we go now?’ ‘No, let's wait until it stops raining.’  or  … till it stops raining.
• I was very tired this morning. I stayed in bed until half past ten.
until (situation continues up to a point)

Joe will be away until Monday.
(so he will return on Monday)

I'll be working until 11.30.
(so I will stop working at 11.30)

by (happens not later than a point)

Joe will be back by Monday.
(= not later than Monday)

I'll have finished my work by 11.30.
(= I will finish not later than 11.30)

Part B: By the Time Something Happens (Future)

You can say by the time something happens:

• It's too late to go to the bank now. By the time we get there, it will be closed.
• You'll need plenty of time at the airport. By the time you check in and go through security, it will be time for your flight.
• Hurry up! By the time we get to the cinema, the film will already have started.

Part C: By the Time Something Happened (Past)

You can say by the time something happened (for past sequences, often with past perfect):

• Karen's car broke down on the way to the party last night. By the time she arrived, most of the other guests had left.
• I had a lot of work to do yesterday evening. I was very tired by the time I finished.
• We went to the cinema last night. It took us a long time to find somewhere to park. By the time we got to the cinema, the film had already started.

You can also say by then or by that time:

Karen finally got to the party at midnight, but by then most of the other guests had left.  or  … but by that time, most of the other guests had left.

Exercise 120.4 Read the situations and complete the sentences using By the time … .
  • I was invited to a party, but I got there much later than I intended.
    , most of the other guests had left.
  • I intended to catch a train, but it took me longer than expected to get to the station.
    , my train had already left.
  • I wanted to go shopping after work. But I finished work much later than expected.
    , it was too late to go shopping.
  • I saw two men who looked as if they were trying to steal a car. I called the police, but it was some time before they arrived.
    , the two men had disappeared.
  • We climbed a mountain and it took us a long time to get to the top. There wasn't much time to enjoy the view.
    , we had to come down again.

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 120

Category Rule Correct Example
by … (= not later than)Specifies an event's deadline or limitThey should arrive by Monday. We have to be home by 5.
until / tillStates how long a continuous situation or activity lastsWait until it stops raining. Joe will be away until Monday.
by the time + clause (future)Something will already have completed before that pointBy the time we get there, it will be closed.
by the time + clause (past)Something had already happened before a past eventBy the time she arrived, the guests had left.
by then / by that timeAdverbial shorthand for an earlier completed actionWe got there at midnight, but by then it had started.
