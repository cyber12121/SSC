Unit 119 • Conjunctions and prepositions

during, for and whiletime expressions: during (+ noun), for (+ duration), while (+ clause)

We use during + noun to say when something happens within a period. We use for + time period to say how long something lasts. We use while + subject + verb for simultaneous actions.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use during + noun to indicate when an event occurs: I fell asleep during the movie.
  • Use for + duration to specify how long an action continues: It rained for three days.
  • Distinguish between during (answers ‘When?’) and for (answers ‘How long?’).
  • Use while + subject + verb for simultaneous background clauses: while we were on holiday.
  • Avoid using will after while when referring to future events (use present simple).

Part A — Theory

Part A: During (+ Noun)

during = at a time between the start and end of something:

• I fell asleep during the movie. (= at a time between start and end of the movie)
• We met some really nice people during our holiday.
• The ground is wet. It must have rained during the night.

With ‘time words’ (the morning, the night, the summer etc.), you can usually say in or during:

It rained in the night.  or  … during the night.
It's lovely here during the summer.  or  … in the summer.

Part B: For vs During

We use for (+ a period of time) to say how long something goes on:

• We watched TV for two hours last night.
• Jess is going away for a week in September.
• How are you? I haven't seen you for ages.
• Are you going away for the weekend?
Crucial Rule: Do not use during to say how long something goes on:
It rained for three days without stopping. (NOT during three days)
during (When did it happen?)

‘When did you fall asleep?’
‘During the movie.’

for (How long did it last?)

‘How long were you asleep?’
‘For half an hour.’

Part C: During (+ Noun) vs While (+ Subject + Verb)

during + noun

I fell asleep during the movie.

We met a lot of interesting people during our holiday.

Robert suddenly began to feel ill during the exam.

while + subject + verb

I fell asleep while I was watching TV.

We met a lot of interesting people while we were on holiday.

Robert suddenly began to feel ill while he was doing the exam.

When we are talking about the future, we use the present tense after while. Do not use will:

• I'm going to Moscow next week. I hope the weather will be good while I'm there. (NOT while I will be)
• What are you going to do while you're waiting? (NOT while you'll be waiting)
