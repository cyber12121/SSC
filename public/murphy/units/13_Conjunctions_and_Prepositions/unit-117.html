Unit 117 • Conjunctions and prepositions

like and assimilarity, roles, manner: like (preposition) vs as (conjunction & role)

We use like to express similarity ('similar to / the same as') followed by a noun, pronoun, or -ing. We use as with a clause (subject + verb) or as a preposition meaning 'in the role/position of'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use prepositional like (+ noun, pronoun, or -ing) to express similarity: It's like a palace / like walking on ice.
  • Give examples using like or such as: water sports, like surfing.
  • Use as (+ subject + verb) to mean in the same way as: I left everything as it was.
  • Employ fixed expressions with as: as usual, as always, the same as, as you know, as I said.
  • Distinguish between prepositional like (comparison/analogy: He works like a slave) and prepositional as (real role/job: She works as a tour guide).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Like = Similar To, The Same As

We use like to mean ‘similar to’ or ‘the same as’:

• What a beautiful house! It's like a palace. (NOT as a palace)
• Be careful! The floor has been polished. It's like walking on ice. (NOT as walking)
• It's raining again. I hate weather like this. (NOT as this)
• ‘What's that noise?’ ‘It sounds like a baby crying.’ (NOT as a baby crying)

In these examples, like is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun (like a palace), a pronoun (like this) or -ing (like walking).

Sometimes like = ‘for example’. You can also use such as:

I enjoy water sports, like surfing, scuba diving and water-skiing.  or  … such as surfing …

Part B: As = In the Same Way As (Subject + Verb)

As = in the same way as, in the same condition as. We use as with subject (S) + verb (V):

• I didn't move anything. I left everything as it was.
• You should have done it as I showed you.

In informal English, people also use like in this way (+ subject + verb):

I left everything like it was.  or  You should have done it like I showed you.

Compare as and like:

as / like + S + V

You should have done it as I showed you.
or … like I showed you.

like + pronoun / noun

You should have done it like this.
(NOT as this)

Notice these set expressions:

• You're late as usual. • As always, Nick was the first to complain.
• Your phone is the same as mine. (NOT the same like)

Part C: As You Know, As I Said, As Expected

After verbs like do, say, know, think, expect, we use as (+ S + V):

• You can do as you like. (= do what you like)
• They did as they promised. (= they did what they promised)
• As you know, it's Emma's birthday next week. (= you know this already)
• Andy failed his driving test, as he expected. (= he expected this before)
• As I said yesterday, I'm sure we can solve the problem.  or  Like I said yesterday …

Part D: As as a Preposition (= In the Role / Form Of)

As can also be a preposition (as + noun), but the meaning is fundamentally different from like:

as a taxi driver (Real job)

As a taxi driver, I spend most of my working life in a car.
(= I really am a taxi driver; it is my actual occupation.)

like a taxi driver (Comparison)

Everyone in my family wants me to drive them everywhere. I'm like a taxi driver.
(= I am NOT really a taxi driver, but I resemble one.)

More examples of as + noun (preposition):

• Many years ago I worked as a photographer. (= I was a photographer)
• Many words can be used as verbs or nouns.
• London is fine as a place to visit, but I wouldn't like to live there.
• The news of the tragedy came as a great shock.
