Unit 79 • Articles and nouns

Singular and pluralTwo-part nouns (trousers/glasses), news, series/species, collective nouns

Some nouns are always plural (trousers, scissors, glasses) and take a plural verb. Some nouns end in -s but are singular (news, physics, athletics). Collective nouns (government, staff, team) can take singular or plural verbs.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master two-part nouns that are always plural: trousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas, glasses, binoculars, scissors.
  • Learn nouns ending in -s that are singular: news, mathematics/maths, physics, athletics, economics.
  • Identify nouns with identical singular and plural forms: means, series, species.
  • Understand collective nouns (government, team, family, police) and sums of money/time taking singular verbs (three years is a long time).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Two-Part Plural Nouns

Sometimes we use a plural noun for one thing that has two parts. These words take a plural verb:

Clothes: trousers, jeans, tights, shorts, pants, pyjamas
Tools & Optical: glasses, binoculars, scissors
My trousers are too long. (not My trousers is)
Those are nice jeans.  or  That’s a nice pair of jeans. (not a nice jeans)
I need some scissors / a pair of scissors.

Part B: Singular Nouns Ending in -s; Means / Series / Species

Some nouns end in -s but are singular:

news: The news was unexpected. (not The news were)
games: gymnastics, billiards, darts, dominoes
subjects/activities: physics, maths, economics, politics, athletics: Physics was my favourite subject.

Some nouns end in -s and can be either singular or plural:

means (= a method): a means of transport / many means of transport
series: a television series / two television series
species: a species of bird / 200 species of birds

Part C: Collective Nouns (Government, Team, Police)

Some nouns can take either a singular or a plural verb depending on whether we think of the group as a single whole or individual members:

audience • committee • company • family • government • staff • team
The government wants (or want) to improve education.
The team is (or are) playing well.

Warning: The police is ALWAYS plural and takes a plural verb:

The police are investigating the murder. (not The police is)
A police officer / a policeman / a policewoman (for one individual).

Person / People: He is a nice person. → They are nice people. (not persons).

Part D: Sums of Money, Periods of Time, Distances

We use a singular verb with sums of money, periods of time, and distances because we think of them as a single amount/unit:

Twenty thousand pounds was stolen in the robbery. (not were stolen)
Three years is a long time to be without a job. (not are a long time)
Six miles is a long way to walk every day.
