Unit 80 • Articles and nouns

Noun + noun (a bus driver / a headache)Compound nouns, classifying first noun, singular first noun in measurements

You can use two nouns together (noun + noun) where the first noun acts like an adjective describing the second. Notice that the first noun is usually singular (a shoe shop, a three-hour flight).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Understand how compound nouns work (noun + noun) where the first noun classifies the second (a bus driver, income tax, a shoe shop).
  • Identify the critical rule: the first noun is almost always singular even if its meaning is plural (a toothbrush, not *teethbrush; a chocolate box).
  • Learn hyphenated compound measurements where the first noun has no plural -s (a two-hour flight, a 15-minute break, a six-year-old girl).
  • Contrast a race horse (a horse) vs a horse race (a race).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Using Two Nouns Together (Noun + Noun)

You can use two nouns together (noun + noun) to mean one thing, person, or idea:

a bus driver = the driver of a bus
income tax = tax that you pay on your income
the city centre = the centre of the city
an apple tree = a tree that has apples
a Paris hotel = a hotel in Paris

The first noun is like an adjective – it tells us what kind of thing, person, or idea it is.

Notice how the meaning changes if you swap the words:

a race horse = a horse that runs in races
a horse race = a race between horses

Part B: The First Noun is Normally Singular

The first noun is normally singular, even if the meaning is plural:

a bookshop (a shop that sells books, not *a booksshop)
an eye test (a test for your eyes)
a toothbrush (a brush for your teeth)
a shoe shop (not *a shoes shop)
a car factory (a factory that makes cars)

Part C: Compound Measurements (a two-hour flight)

In compound expressions with a number + noun, the noun is singular (usually with a hyphen):

Compound Adjective (Singular)

a two-hour flight
a ten-pound note
a 15-minute walk
a twelve-storey building
a six-year-old child

Normal Noun Phrase (Plural)

The flight took two hours.
It costs ten pounds.
The walk took 15 minutes.
The building has twelve storeys.
The child is six years old.
