Unit 78 • Articles and nouns

Names with and without the 2Streets, buildings, airports, stations, universities, hotels, museums

Most streets, airports, stations, and universities do not take 'the'. Names of hotels, restaurants, theatres, cinemas, and museums usually take 'the' (unless named after the owner with -'s).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Learn why city streets, roads, squares, and parks do NOT take the (Fifth Avenue, Broadway, Times Square, Hyde Park).
  • Master the rule for institutions named after places/people (Manchester Airport, Harvard University, Victoria Station – no the).
  • Identify buildings that take the: hotels, restaurants, theatres, cinemas, museums (the Hilton, the National Theatre, the British Museum).
  • Contrast names with apostrophe -'s (Blackstone's, Mario's, St Paul's Cathedral – no the).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Streets, Squares, Parks (No 'the')

We do not use the with names of most city streets, roads, squares, and parks:

Union Street • Fifth Avenue • Hyde Park • Broadway • Times Square • Abbey Road

Names of many public buildings and institutions (airports, stations, universities etc.) consist of two words where the first is a place or person's name – these do not take the:

Manchester Airport • Heathrow Airport • Victoria Station • Edinburgh Castle • Harvard University • Cambridge University
Compare: the University of Cambridge (with of, we use the).

Part B: Hotels, Theatres, Museums (With 'the')

We normally use the with the names of hotels, restaurants, pubs, theatres, cinemas, and museums:

Hotels: the Hilton, the Sheraton, the Grand Hotel
Theatres: the National Theatre, the Globe
Cinemas: the Odeon, the Classic
Museums: the British Museum, the Tate Gallery
Monuments: the Eiffel Tower, the White House, the Statue of Liberty

Part C: Names with -'s (No 'the')

We do not use the with names of places or shops named after a person with -'s:

McDonald's • Harrods • Macy's • Barclays • St Paul's Cathedral • St John's Church
Compare: the Science Museum (not named after a person with -'s).

Part D: Newspapers & Companies

Newspapers: Names of newspapers usually take the: the Times, the Washington Post, the Guardian.

Companies & Airlines: Companies, banks, and airlines do not usually take the: Apple, Sony, British Airways, Cathay Pacific, IBM.
