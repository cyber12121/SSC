Unit 81 • Articles and nouns

-'s (your sister's name) and of ... (the name of the book)Possessive -'s for people/animals vs 'of' for things, time expressions

We use -'s (apostrophe + s) mostly for people or animals. For things, we normally use 'of' (the door of the car). We also use -'s with time expressions (yesterday's news, two weeks' holiday).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master when to use -'s (mostly people, animals, organizations: Tom's sister, the cat's tail).
  • Use of ... for things and parts of things (the door of the car, the name of the book).
  • Learn rules for singular -'s (James's / Chris's) vs plural -s' (my parents' car) vs irregular plurals (children's books).
  • Use -'s with time expressions (yesterday's news, two weeks' holiday, twenty minutes' walk).

Part A — Theory

Part A: When to Use -'s (People and Animals)

We use -'s (apostrophe + s) mostly for people or animals:

Tom’s computer isn’t working. (not the computer of Tom)
How old are Chris’s children? (not the children of Chris)
What’s your sister’s name?
Be careful. Don’t step on the cat’s tail.

You can use -'s without a noun after it:

This isn’t my book. It’s my sister’s. (= my sister's book)

With names ending in -s, you can add -'s: James's daughter / Chris's children.

Part B: Singular -'s vs Plural -s'

Singular noun + -'s

my sister’s room (one sister)
the boy’s dog (one boy)
a woman’s hat

Plural noun + -s'

my sisters’ room (more than one sister)
the boys’ dog (more than one boy)
our neighbours’ garden

Irregular plurals: If the plural noun does not end in -s, add -'s:

the men’s changing room • children’s books • women’s clothes • people’s opinions

Part C: Things: Usually 'of ...'

For things, ideas etc., we normally use of … (not -'s):

the door of the car (not the car’s door)
the name of the book (not the book’s name)
the top of the page • the cost of living • the cause of the problem

You can also use -'s with organizations (a group of people):

the government’s policy  or  the policy of the government
the company’s decision  or  the decision of the company

Part D: Time Expressions with -'s

We use -'s with time expressions (yesterday, today, tomorrow, this evening etc.):

Did you see yesterday’s newspaper?
Tomorrow’s meeting has been cancelled.
last week’s storm caused a lot of damage.

We also use -'s / -s' with periods of time:

I’ve got a week’s holiday next month.
It’s about twenty minutes’ walk from here.
I need an hour’s sleep.
