Unit 75 • Articles and nouns

the 3 (children / the children)General statements (no the) vs specific groups (with the)

When talking about things or people in general, we do NOT use 'the'. We use 'the' only when referring to a specific, identified group or item.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the critical rule: do NOT use the when talking about things or people in general (I'm afraid of dogs, not *the dogs).
  • Identify general categories without the: sports (football, tennis), subjects (history, physics), languages, meals, abstract concepts (life, war).
  • Correct common errors with most: say most people / most shops (not *the most people).
  • Contrast generic plural/uncountable nouns with specific groups taking the (children vs the children).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Things or People in General (No 'the')

When we are talking about things or people in general, we do not use the:

I’m afraid of dogs. (not the dogs)
(dogs = dogs in general, not a specific group of dogs)
Doctors are usually paid more than teachers.
Do you know anybody who collects stamps?
Life has changed a lot in the last thirty years. (not The life)
Do you like classical music / Chinese food / fast cars?
My favourite sport is football / skiing / athletics.
My favourite subject at school was history / physics / English.

We say most people / most shops / most big cities etc. (not the most …):

Most shops accept credit cards. (not The most shops)

Part B: General vs Specific (children vs the children)

We use the when we mean specific things or people. Compare:

In General (no 'the')

Children learn from playing.
(children in general)

I couldn’t live without music.
(music in general)

All cars have wheels.
(all cars in the world)

Sugar isn’t very good for you.
(sugar in general)

English people drink a lot of tea.
(English people in general)

Specific (with 'the')

We took the children to the zoo.
(a specific group: our children)

The film was good, but I didn’t like the music.
(the music in that film)

All the cars in this car park belong to staff.
(specific cars parked here)

Can you pass the sugar, please?
(the sugar on the table)

The English people I know drink a lot of tea.
(only the ones I know)
