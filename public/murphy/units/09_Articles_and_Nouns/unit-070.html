Unit 70 • Articles and nouns

Countable and uncountable 2Dual-meaning nouns (noise/paper/room) & inherently uncountable nouns

Many nouns can be countable in one meaning and uncountable in another. In addition, English has common nouns that are always uncountable and never take -s or a/an.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Identify nouns that can be countable or uncountable depending on meaning: noise, paper, hair, room, experience, time.
  • Learn common drinks used countably: a coffee (= a cup of coffee), two teas.
  • Master inherently uncountable English nouns that are often incorrectly pluralized: accommodation, advice, baggage/luggage, bread, chaos, furniture, information, news, permission, progress, scenery, traffic, weather, work.
  • Distinguish countable job, view, trip/journey from uncountable work, scenery, travel.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Nouns with Countable and Uncountable Meanings

Many nouns are sometimes countable, and sometimes uncountable. Usually there is a difference in meaning. Compare:

Countable Meaning
  • Did you hear a noise just now? (= a specific sound)
  • I bought a paper to read. (= a newspaper)
  • There’s a hair in my soup! (= one single individual hair)
  • This is a nice room. (= a room in a building)
  • I had some interesting experiences while travelling. (= events/things that happened to me)
  • Enjoy your trip. Have a great time!
Uncountable Meaning
  • I can’t work here. There’s too much noise. (= noise in general)
  • I need some paper to write on. (= material for writing on)
  • You’ve got very long hair. (not hairs) (= all hair on your head)
  • You can’t sit here. There isn’t room. (= space)
  • I got the job because I had a lot of experience. (not experiences) (= work knowledge)
  • I can’t wait. I don’t have time.
Drinks (Coffee, Tea, Juice, Beer) Drinks are normally uncountable: I don’t like coffee very much.
But you can say a coffee (= a cup of coffee), two coffees (= two cups):
Two coffees and an orange juice, please.

Part B: Nouns that are Usually Uncountable

These nouns are usually uncountable in English:

accommodation
advice
baggage
behaviour
bread
chaos
damage
furniture
information
luck
luggage
news
permission
progress
scenery
traffic
weather
work

Rules to remember:

  • We do not normally use a/an with these: I’m going to buy some bread or a loaf of bread (not a bread). I hope you have good weather (not a good weather).
  • These nouns are not plural: Where are you going to put all your furniture? (not furnitures). Let me know if you need more information (not informations).
  • News is uncountable and takes a singular verb: The news was unexpected (not The news were).
  • Travel means 'travelling in general' (uncountable). We do not say 'a travel' for a trip: They spend a lot of money on travel. but We had a very good trip/journey (not a good travel).

Countable vs Uncountable Equivalents:

Countable:
I’m looking for a job.
What a beautiful view!
It’s a nice day today.
We had a lot of bags.
These chairs are mine.
That’s a good suggestion.
There were a lot of cars.

Uncountable:
I’m looking for work. (not a work)
What beautiful scenery! (not a scenery)
It’s nice weather today.
We had a lot of baggage/luggage.
This furniture is mine. (not furnitures)
That’s good advice. (not an advice)
There was a lot of traffic.
