Unit 72 • Articles and nouns

a/an and thea/an (general, first mention) vs the (specific, already known)

We use a/an when talking about something for the first time, or when it's not specific. We use 'the' when both speaker and listener know which specific thing is meant.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the distinction between a/an (first mention, non-specific) and the (specific, known to speaker and listener).
  • Identify situational uses of the: parts of a room/building (the light, the door, the floor) and public amenities (the bank, the post office, the station, the doctor).
  • Contrast generic rates: once a week, three times a day, £1.50 a kilo.
  • Compare a ... (what kind of thing) vs the ... (specific thing).

Part A — Theory

Part A: First Mention vs Known Specific Thing

Study this introductory dialogue example:

Joe: "I had a sandwich and an apple for lunch. The sandwich wasn’t very good, but the apple was nice."
  • Joe says a sandwich and an apple because this is the first time he mentions them.
  • Joe then says the sandwich and the apple because we now know which sandwich and which apple he means – the ones he had for lunch.
Further Examples A man and a woman were sitting opposite me. The man was American, but I think the woman was British.
When we were on holiday, we stayed at a hotel. Sometimes we ate at the hotel and sometimes we went to a restaurant.

Part B: Specific Thing vs General / Kind of Thing

We use the when we are thinking of a specific thing. Compare a/an and the:

a / an (any one / non-specific)

Tim sat down on a chair.
(maybe one of many chairs in the room)

Do you have a car?
(any car / not a specific one)

We stayed at a very cheap hotel.
(describing what kind of hotel)

the (a specific one)

Tim sat down on the chair nearest the door.
(a particular chair)

I cleaned the car yesterday.
(= my car)

The hotel where we stayed was very cheap.
(that specific hotel)

Part C: Situational 'the' (Clear from context)

We use the when it is clear which thing or person we mean:

  • In a room: the light, the floor, the ceiling, the door, the carpet etc.:
    Can you turn off the light, please? (= the light in this room)
  • In a town: the station, the airport, the centre:
    I took a taxi to the station. (= the station in that town)
  • In a shop: I’d like to speak to the manager, please. (= the manager of this shop)

We also say (go to) the bank / the post office and the doctor / the dentist:

I have to go to the bank and then I’m going to the post office.
Clare isn’t well. She’s gone to the doctor.
I don’t like going to the dentist.

I have to go to the bank today.
I don’t like going to the dentist.

Is there a bank near here?
My sister is a dentist.

Part D: Rates and Frequencies (once a week, £1.50 a kilo)

We say once a week / three times a day / £1.50 a kilo etc.:

I go to the cinema about once a month.
"How much are those potatoes?" "£1.50 a kilo."
Helen works eight hours a day, six days a week.

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 72.1 Put in a/an or the.
  • This morning I bought book and magazine. book is in my bag, but I can't remember where I put magazine.
  • I saw accident this morning. car crashed into tree. driver of car wasn't hurt, but car was badly damaged.
  • There are two cars parked outside: blue one and grey one. blue one belongs to my neighbours. I don't know who owner of grey one is.
  • My friends live in old house in small village. There is beautiful garden behind house. I would like to have garden like that.
Exercise 72.2 Put in a/an or the. Choose the correct article for each sentence.
  • (a) This house is very nice. Does it have garden?
    (b) It's a beautiful day. Let's sit in garden.
    (c) I like living in this house, but it's a shame that garden is so small.
  • (a) Can you recommend good restaurant?
    (b) We had dinner in very nice restaurant.
    (c) We had dinner in best restaurant in town.
  • (a) What's name of that man we met yesterday?
    (b) We stayed at a very nice hotel – I can't remember name now.
    (c) My neighbour has French name, but in fact she's English.
  • (a) Did Paula get job she applied for?
    (b) It's not easy to get job at the moment.
    (c) Do you enjoy your work? Is it interesting job?
  • (a) 'Are you going away next week?' 'No, week after next.'
    (b) I'm going away for week in September.
    (c) Gary has a part-time job. He works three mornings week.
