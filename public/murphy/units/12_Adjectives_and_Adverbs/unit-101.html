Unit 101 • Adjectives and adverbs

Adjectives and adverbs 2 (well, fast, late, hard/hardly)good vs well; dual adjective/adverbs (fast, hard, late); hard vs hardly; hardly any/anything

While 'good' is an adjective, 'well' is its adverb (and an adjective meaning healthy). Words like fast, hard, and late share the same adjective and adverb form. 'Hardly' means almost not at all.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Contrast the adjective good with the adverb well (and recognize well meaning healthy).
  • Identify dual-function words that are both adjectives and adverbs: fast, hard, late.
  • Distinguish between hard (with great effort) and hardly (almost not at all).
  • Use hardly with quantifiers and pronouns: hardly any, hardly anything, hardly ever.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Good and Well

Good is an adjective • The adverb is well:

Your English is good.
Sophie is a good pianist.

You speak English well.
Sophie plays the piano well.

We use well with past participles: well-known • well-educated • well-paid • well-behaved.

Well is also an adjective meaning 'in good health': ‘How are you?’ ‘I'm very well, thanks.’

Part B: Fast, Hard and Late

These words are both adjectives and adverbs:

Adjective

Darren is a fast runner.
It's hard to find a job right now.
Sorry I'm late.

Adverb

Darren can run fast.
Kate works hard. (not works hardly)
I got up late.

Note: lately = recently: Have you seen Kate lately?

Part C: Hard vs Hardly

Hardly = very little, almost not:

Sarah wasn't friendly. She hardly spoke to me. (= spoke very little)
We've only met once. We hardly know each other.
Your writing is terrible. I can hardly read it. (= almost impossible to read)
hard (= with a lot of effort)

He tried hard to find a job, but had no luck.

hardly (= almost not at all)

He hardly tried to find a job. (= very little effort)

Hardly precedes the verb: We hardly know each other. (not know each other hardly)

Part D: Hardly Any / Anything / Ever

You can use hardly with any, anybody, anyone, anything, anywhere:

How much money do we have? Hardly any. (= almost none)
The exam was tough. Hardly anybody passed. (= very few people)
She was very quiet. She said hardly anything.
I'm almost always at home. I hardly ever go out. (= almost never)

Hardly also means 'certainly not': It's hardly surprising that you're tired.
