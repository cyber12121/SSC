Unit 107 • Adjectives and adverbs

Comparative 3 (as … as / than)as … as equality & not as … as inequality; twice as … as; the same as; than me vs than I am

We use 'as … as' to show that two things are equal, and 'not as … as' (or 'less than') to show that they are not equal. We say 'the same as' (not 'the same like').

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use not as … as (and less than) to show inequality: He isn't as rich as Sarah.
  • Use as … as in positive sentences, questions, and multipliers: twice as expensive as.
  • Use the same as (never the same like).
  • Identify object pronoun vs subject pronoun + auxiliary after than/as: taller than me or taller than I am.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Not as … as vs Less than

Study this example:

Sarah has $20 million, Joe has $15 million, and David has $10 million.
• Joe is rich.
• He is richer than David.
• But he isn't as rich as Sarah. (= Sarah is richer than he is)
Jack isn't as old as he looks. (= he looks older than he is)
The town centre wasn't as crowded as usual.
Lisa didn't do as well in the exam as she had hoped.
I don't know as many people as you do.

Less than is similar to not as … as:

I spent less money than you. (= I didn't spend as much money as you)
The city was less crowded than usual. (= wasn't as crowded as usual)

Part B: As … as • Twice as … as

We use as … as in positive statements and questions:

I'm sorry I'm late. I got here as fast as I could.
You can have as much as you want.
Can you send the info as soon as possible?
Let's walk. It's just as quick as taking the bus.

With multipliers (twice as … as, three times as … as):

Petrol is twice as expensive as it was a few years ago.
Their house is about three times as big as ours.

Part C: The Same As

We say the same as (not the same like):

Laura's salary is the same as mine.
David is the same age as James.
She still looks the same as she did ten years ago.

Part D: Than Me / Than I Am

After than and as, both object pronouns and subject + verb are standard:

Object Pronoun

You're taller than me.
He's not as clever as her.
They have more money than us.
I can't run as fast as him.

Subject + Auxiliary Verb

You're taller than I am.
He's not as clever as she is.
They have more money than we have.
I can't run as fast as he can.
