Unit 103 • Adjectives and adverbs

enough and tooposition of enough (after adjectives/adverbs, before nouns); too vs enough; too/enough + for/to

We use 'enough' after adjectives and adverbs, but before nouns. 'Too' means more than necessary or desired. Both can be followed by 'for somebody' or 'to do something'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Position enough correctly: after adjectives and adverbs (fit enough, long enough), but before nouns (enough money, enough chairs).
  • Contrast too … (more than necessary) with not … enough (less than necessary).
  • Construct patterns with for somebody and to do something (too small for you, warm enough to sit outside).
  • Avoid pronoun repetition at the end of infinitive clauses: say The food was too hot to eat (not to eat it).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Position of Enough

Enough goes after adjectives and adverbs:

I can't run very far. I'm not fit enough. (not enough fit)
Let's go. We've waited long enough.
Is your coffee warm enough?

Enough normally goes before nouns:

We have enough money. We don't need any more.
There weren't enough chairs. Some of us had to sit on the floor.

You can also use enough alone (without a noun or adjective):

We don't need more money. We have enough.

Part B: Too vs Not … Enough

too (= more than is necessary)

You never stop working. You work too hard.
There's too much furniture in this room.
There were too many people.

not … enough (= less than necessary)

You're lazy. You don't work hard enough.
There's not enough space.
There were not enough chairs.

Part C: Enough/Too with For and To

We say enough / too … for somebody/something:

Does Joe have enough experience for the job?
This bag isn't big enough for all my clothes.
That shirt is too small for you.

We say enough / too … to do something:

Does Joe have enough experience to do the job?
It's too far to walk home from here.
She's not old enough to have a driving licence.
The bridge is just wide enough for two cars to pass one another.

Part D: No Pronoun Repetition ('Too Hot to Eat')

Notice the structure at the end of the sentence – do not repeat the pronoun:

The food was very hot. We couldn't eat it.
and The food was so hot that we couldn't eat it.
but The food was too hot to eat. (not to eat it)
These boxes are too heavy to carry. (not to carry them)
The wallet was too big to put in my pocket. (not to put it)
This chair isn't strong enough to stand on. (not to stand on it)
