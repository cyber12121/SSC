Unit 99 • Adjectives and adverbs

Adjectives: a nice new house, you look tiredorder of adjectives (opinion before fact, size/age/shape/colour/origin/material) & adjectives after linking verbs

When using multiple adjectives, opinion adjectives precede fact adjectives. After linking verbs (look, feel, sound, taste, smell, be, become, seem), we use adjectives rather than adverbs.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Order adjectives correctly: opinion adjectives (nice, beautiful) before fact adjectives (new, wooden).
  • Sequence fact adjectives by standard category: 1. Size/length → 2. Age → 3. Colour → 4. Origin → 5. Material.
  • Use and with two or more colour adjectives (a black and white dress).
  • Use adjectives (not adverbs) after linking verbs: look, feel, sound, taste, smell, seem, become (You look tired).
  • Correctly order fixed time phrases: the first two days, the next few weeks.

Part A — Theory

Part A: Opinion vs Fact Adjectives

Sometimes we use two or more adjectives together:

My brother lives in a nice new house.
In the kitchen there was a beautiful large round wooden table.

Opinion adjectives (nice, beautiful, lovely, delicious) tell what the speaker thinks. They go before fact adjectives:

a nice long summer holiday • an interesting young man • delicious hot vegetable soup

Part B: Order of Fact Adjectives

Usually we put fact adjectives in this order:

1. How big?
large, tall, small
2. How old?
young, old, new
3. What colour?
black, blue, red
4. Where from?
French, Russian
5. What made of?
wooden, plastic
a tall young man (1 → 2)
a large wooden table (1 → 5)
big blue eyes (1 → 3)
an old Russian song (2 → 4)
a small black plastic bag (1 → 3 → 5)
an old white cotton shirt (2 → 3 → 5)

Size/length precedes shape/width: a large round table • a tall thin girl • a long narrow street.

When there are two colour adjectives, we use and: a black and white dress • a red, white and green flag.

Part C: Adjectives After Linking Verbs

We use adjectives (not adverbs) after be, get, become, seem and sensory verbs (look, feel, sound, taste, smell):

You look tired. / I feel tired. / She sounds tired.
The dinner smells good.
This tea tastes strange. (not tastes strangely)
Your friend seems very nice.

Contrast with action verbs which require an adverb:

Linking Verb + Adjective

You look tired.
The music sounds beautiful.
The soup tastes good.

Action Verb + Adverb

Drive carefully! (not Drive careful)
Suzanne plays the piano well.
He walked slowly.

Part D: 'The First Two Days', 'The Next Few Weeks'

Ordinal/sequence numbers precede cardinal numbers:

I didn't enjoy the first two days of the course. (not the two first days)
They'll be away for the next few weeks. (not the few next weeks)
We enjoyed the last three days of our holiday.
