Unit 26 • Tenses Basics

can, could and (be) able toAbility, possibility, permission

Learn how to use can and be able to for ability, and how to choose between could and managed to in the past.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use can for general ability and permission in the present.
  • Use (be) able to instead of can (especially with perfect tenses, infinitives, etc.).
  • Use could for general past ability, and was/were able to or managed to for specific past achievements.

Part A — Theory

A. can and (be) able to

We use can to say that something is possible or that somebody has the ability to do something:

  • We can see the lake from our hotel room.
  • I don't have a pen. Can you lend me one?
  • Can you speak any foreign languages?

You can use be able to instead of can, but can is more usual in the present:

  • Are you able to speak any foreign languages? (more usual: Can you speak ... ?)

can has only two forms: can (present) and could (past). Sometimes you MUST use (be) able to:

  • I haven't been able to sleep recently. (can has no present perfect)
  • Nicole might be able to help you. (can has no infinitive)

B. could and was/were able to (Past)

could is used for general ability in the past:

  • My grandfather could speak five languages.
  • We were totally free. We could do what we wanted.

Important distinction for a specific situation:

If you want to say that somebody did something in a specific situation, use was/were able to or managed to (not could):

• The fire spread quickly, but everybody was able to escape. (not could escape)

• They didn't want to come with us at first, but we managed to persuade them.

In negative sentences, couldn't is possible in all situations:

  • My grandfather couldn't swim. (general)
  • We tried hard, but we couldn't persuade them. (specific situation)

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 26

Category Rule Correct Example
can vs be able to can for present ability; be able to after modals and perfect forms I can swim. I haven't been able to sleep. She might be able to help.
could (general) vs managed to (specific) could = general ability; managed to / was able to = specific achievement He could speak French. The fire spread, but he managed to escape.
couldn't in the past Use couldn't for both general and specific failures I couldn't find my keys. She couldn't walk well.
