Unit 29 • Tenses Basics

may and might 1Possibility in present and past

Learn how to express possibility with may and might, and how to deduce past possibilities with might have.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use may and might to say something is possible in the present or future.
  • Use may have / might have to talk about possibilities in the past.
  • Form negatives with may not and might not, contrasting with couldn't.

Part A — Theory

A. may and might for present and future possibility

We use may or might to say that something is possible:

  • It may be true. or It might be true. (= perhaps it is true)
  • She might know. or She may know.

The negative forms are may not and might not:

  • It may not be true. (= perhaps it is not true)
  • I'm not sure whether I can lend you any money. I might not have enough.

B. may have and might have (Past)

For the past we use may have (done) or might have (done):

• 'I can't find my phone.' 'You might have left it at work.' (= perhaps you left it)

• 'Why didn't David answer the door?' 'He may have been asleep.'

Compare might not have and couldn't have:

  • She might not have known about the meeting. (= perhaps she didn't know)
  • She couldn't have known about the meeting. (= it was impossible for her to know)

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 29

Category Rule Correct Example
may / might (present) Something is possible (perhaps it is true) It may be true. She might know.
may / might have (past) Possibility about something that happened in the past She may have forgotten. He might have been asleep.
might not vs couldn't might not = perhaps not; couldn't = impossible He might not have wanted to come (perhaps). It couldn't have been an accident (impossible).
