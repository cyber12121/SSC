Unit 31 • Tenses Basics

have to and mustObligation and necessity

Learn when to use have to for external obligations and must for personal decisions, and master the contrast between mustn't and don't have to.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use have to for obligations that come from facts, rules, or other people.
  • Use must for personal feelings of necessity from the speaker.
  • Recognize past (had to), question, and negative forms (don't have to vs mustn't).

Part A — Theory

A. have to (do)

I have to do something = it is necessary to do it, I am obliged to do it:

  • You can't turn right here. You have to turn left. (because of the traffic system)
  • I have to wear glasses for reading.
  • Robert can't come out with us. He has to work late.

For the past (yesterday / last week etc.) we use had to:

  • We were late because we had to wait for the bus. (not had must)

In questions and negative sentences we use do/does/did:

  • What time do you have to go to work tomorrow?
  • Why did they have to leave so early?
  • I'm not working tomorrow, so I don't have to get up early.

B. must vs have to

must (speaker's personal opinion)

• It's a fantastic film. You must see it.

• I haven't phoned Amy for ages. I must phone her tonight.

have to (facts / external rules)

• In Britain you have to drive on the left.

• I have to get up early tomorrow. My flight is at 7.30.

C. mustn't vs don't have to

mustn't (prohibition)

Do NOT do it; it is necessary NOT to do it:

• You mustn't tell anyone. It's a secret.

• I promised I wouldn't be late. I mustn't be late.

don't have to (no obligation)

You don't need to do it (but you can if you want):

• You don't have to tell him, but you can if you want.

• There's a lift, so we don't have to climb the stairs.

Summary & Quick Reference

Key Takeaways for Unit 31

Category Rule Correct Example
have to vs must have to = rules, facts; must = personal opinion of speaker You have to turn left here (traffic rule). You must read this book (recommendation).
Past Requirement Always had to (must has no past form) I couldn't get a taxi, so I had to walk home.
mustn't vs don't have to mustn't = don't do it (forbidden); don't have to = not necessary You mustn't tell anyone (secret). You don't have to go (optional).
