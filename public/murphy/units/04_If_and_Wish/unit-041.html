Unit 41 • if and wish

wishwishing somebody something, regrets (present & past), and complaints with would

Learn the full range of wish: from good wishes to present/past regrets and complaints using would.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between wish + noun (wishing well) and hope + clause (future expectations).
  • Express present and past regrets using wish + past and wish + past perfect.
  • Use I wish I could and I wish I could have for present and past abilities.
  • Form complaints about annoying habits and desired changes using I wish ... would / wouldn't.

Part A — Theory

A. Wish Somebody Something vs. Hope

We say wish somebody luck / all the best / success / a pleasant stay etc. with a noun:

  • I wish you all the best in the future.
  • I saw Mark before the exam and he wished me luck.

We do not say ‘I wish something happens’. We use hope for things we want to happen in the future:

Wish + Noun Phrase

I wish you a pleasant stay at this hotel.

Hope + Clause

I hope you enjoy your stay at this hotel. (NOT I wish you enjoy)

I'm sorry you're not well. I hope you feel better soon.

B. Wish for Regrets (Present vs. Past)

We also use wish to say that we regret something, that something is not as we would like it:

Present Regret: wish + past simple

I wish I knew what to do. (but I don't know)

I wish you didn't have to go so soon. (but you have to go)

Jack is going to Mexico. I wish I was going too.

Past Regret: wish + past perfect

I wish I'd known about the party. (but I didn't know)

It was a stupid thing to say. I wish I hadn't said it. (but I said it)

C. I wish I could • I wish I could have

  • I wish I could (do something) = I regret that I cannot do it now:
    I'm sorry you have to go. I wish you could stay longer. (but you can't)
  • I wish I could have (done something) = I regret that I could not do it in the past:
    I hear the party was great. I wish I could have gone. (but I couldn't go)

D. I wish … would for Complaints and Changes

We use I wish … would when we want something to happen or change, especially to complain about an annoying situation:

  • It's been raining all day. I wish it would stop raining. (Tanya would like the rain to stop)
  • The phone has been ringing for five minutes. I wish somebody would answer it.
  • I wish you'd do something instead of just sitting there!

We use I wish … wouldn't to complain about repeated annoying habits:

  • I wish you wouldn't keep interrupting me. (= please stop interrupting me)
Important Distinction We use I wish … would for actions we want to happen. We do NOT use would for states or how we want things to be:

I wish Sarah would come.
(= action: I want her to arrive)

I wish Sarah was/were here now.
(NOT I wish Sarah would be here)

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 41.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. wish (example) — wish somebody something.
  2. hope — hope you have (future expectation).
  3. wish — wish you all the best.
  4. wished — wished each other luck.
  5. hope — hope the weather is nice.
  6. wish — wish you every success.
  7. hope — hope it works out well.
Exercise 41.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. I was going (example)
  2. wasn't / weren't — present state regret.
  3. 'd told / had told — past regret: you didn't tell me.
  4. had / could have — present state regret.
  5. could — ability: I wish I could decide.
  6. hadn't bought — past regret about buying shoes.
  7. didn't have — present obligation: wish we didn't have to.
  8. have gone — past ability: wish I could have gone.
Exercise 41.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. I wish it would stop raining. (example)
  2. I wish she would come. (or: ...would hurry up)
  3. I wish somebody would give me a job.
  4. I wish that / the dog would stop barking.
  5. I wish you wouldn't drive so fast. — complaining about an annoying habit.
  6. I wish you wouldn't leave the door open (all the time).
  7. I wish people wouldn't drop litter in the street.
Exercise 41.4 — Key & Explanations
  1. I hadn't said (example)
  2. it would stop (example)
  3. I knew — present state regret.
  4. we hadn't gone — past event regret.
  5. the bus would come — wanting an action/change.
  6. I could come — present/future ability.
  7. it was / were — present state regret.
  8. I'd taken / I had taken — past regret: should have listened.
  9. you'd listen / you would listen — complaint about present behavior.
  10. you wouldn't complain / you didn't complain — complaint.
  11. it wasn't / weren't — present weather state.
  12. the weather would change — wanting weather to improve.
  13. I had / I could have — wishing for possession.
  14. we could have stayed — past ability regret.
