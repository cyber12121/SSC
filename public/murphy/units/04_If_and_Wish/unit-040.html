Unit 40 • if and wish

if I had known … I wish I had known …Past counterfactual conditionals and past regrets with wish

Master third conditional sentences and learn how to express regrets about past events using had done and wish.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Form third conditional sentences using if + past perfect and would have + past participle.
  • Express past regrets using I wish + past perfect.
  • Distinguish between hypothetical present situations (Unit 39) and past counterfactuals (Unit 40).
  • Identify mixed conditionals linking past causes to present effects.

Part A — Theory

A. Third Conditional: if + had ('d) done, would have done

Study this example situation:

Situation Last month Gary was in hospital for a few days. Rachel didn't know this. A few days ago they met by chance. Rachel said:
“If I'd known you were in hospital, I would have gone to visit you.”
(= I didn't know, so I didn't go to visit you. If I'd known = If I had known)

We use if + had ('d) done to talk about something in the past that did not happen:

  • I didn't see you when you passed me in the street. If I'd seen you, I would have said hello.
  • They didn't go out last night. They would have gone out if they hadn't been so tired.
  • If you'd been looking where you were going, you wouldn't have walked into the wall.
  • The view was wonderful. I would have taken some pictures if I'd had (= if I had had) a camera with me.
Present (Unit 39)

I'm not hungry. If I was hungry, I would eat something. (now)

Past (Unit 40)

I wasn't hungry. If I had been hungry, I would have eaten something. (past)

Contraction Note The short form ’d can be would or had:
If I’d seen you (= had seen), I’d have said hello (= would have said).

B. I wish + had done for Past Regrets

We use had done / known / been etc. in the same way after wish to express regret about past actions:

  • I wish I'd known that Gary was ill. I would have gone to see him. (but I didn't know)
  • I feel sick. I wish I hadn't eaten so much. (I ate too much)
  • Do you wish you'd studied science instead of languages? (you didn't study science)
Fact

I'm glad I saw him. (= I saw him)

Regret

I wish I'd seen him. (= I didn't see him)

Do not say ‘wish ... would have’. Say: I wish it had been warmer. (NOT I wish it would have been)

C. would have • could have • might have

Compare how modal auxiliaries change the meaning in past conditionals:

If the weather hadn't been so bad:
  • we would have gone out. (certain result)
  • we could have gone out. (= we would have been able to go out)
  • we might have gone out. (= maybe / perhaps we would have gone out)

Part B — Exercises

Exercise 40.1 9 items • Put the verb into the correct form

Put the verb into the correct form (past perfect, would have done, or would do).

  1. I didn't see you. If (I / see) you, (I / say) hello.
  2. Sarah got to the station just in time to catch her train to the airport. If (she / miss) the train, (she / miss) her flight too.
  3. Thanks for reminding me about Lisa's birthday. (I / forget) if (you / not / remind) me.
  4. I didn't have your email address, so I couldn't contact you. If (I / have) your email address, (I / send) you an email.
  5. Their trip was OK, but (they / enjoy) it more if the weather (be) better.
  6. Sorry we're late. Our taxi got stuck in the traffic. (it / be) quicker if (we / walk).
  7. Why didn't you tell me about your problem? If (you / tell) me, (I / try) to help you.
  8. I'm not tired. If (I / be) tired, I'd go home now.
  9. I wasn't tired last night. If (I / be) tired, I would have gone home earlier.
Exercise 40.2 8 items • Write sentences beginning with If

For each situation, write a conditional sentence beginning with If.

  1. I wasn't hungry, so I didn't eat anything.
  2. The accident happened because the road was icy.
  3. I didn't know that you had to get up early, so I didn't wake you up.
  4. Unfortunately I lost my phone, so I couldn't call you.
  5. Karen wasn't injured in the crash, because fortunately she was wearing a seat belt.
  6. You didn't have any breakfast – that's why you're hungry now.
  7. I didn't get a taxi because I didn't have enough money.
  8. Dan didn't do well at school, so he couldn't go to university.
Exercise 40.3 6 items • Express past regrets with I wish

Imagine that you are in these situations. For each situation, write a sentence with I wish.

  1. You've eaten too much and now you feel sick.
  2. When you were younger, you never learned to play a musical instrument. Now you regret this.
  3. You've painted the gate red. Now you think it doesn't look good. Red was the wrong colour.
  4. You decided to travel by car, but the journey was long and tiring. Going by train would have been better.
  5. Last year you went to New York with a friend. You didn't have time to do all the things you wanted to do.
  6. You moved to a new flat a few months ago. Now you don't like your new flat. You think that moving was a bad idea.

Answers with Full Explanations

Exercise 40.1 — Key & Explanations
  1. I'd seen ... I would have said (example)
  2. she'd missed / she had missed ... she'd have missed / she would have missed
  3. I'd have forgotten / I would have forgotten ... you hadn't reminded
  4. I'd had / I had had ... I'd have sent / I would have sent
  5. they'd have enjoyed / they would have enjoyed ... had been
  6. It would have been / It'd have been ... we'd walked / we had walked
  7. you'd told / you had told ... I'd have tried / I would have tried
  8. I were / I was — present hypothetical: I'd go home now.
  9. I'd been / I had been — past hypothetical: I would have gone home earlier.
Exercise 40.2 — Key & Explanations
  1. If I'd been hungry, I would have eaten something. (example)
  2. If the road hadn't been icy, the accident wouldn't have happened.
  3. If I'd known / If I had known (that you had to get up early), I'd have woken you up.
  4. If I hadn't lost my phone, I'd have called you. (or: ...I would / could have called you)
  5. If Karen hadn't been wearing a seat belt, she'd have been injured. (or: ...might / could have been injured)
  6. If you'd had / If you had had (some) breakfast, you wouldn't be hungry now. (mixed conditional: past action, present result)
  7. If I'd had / If I had had enough money, I'd have got / taken a taxi.
  8. If Dan had done well/better at school, he could/would have gone to university.
Exercise 40.3 — Key & Explanations
  1. I wish I hadn't eaten so much. (example)
  2. I wish I'd learned / had learned to play a musical instrument.
  3. I wish I hadn't painted it red. (or: ...painted the gate red / ...had painted it a different colour)
  4. I wish we'd gone / had gone by train. (or: ...wish we hadn't gone by car)
  5. I wish we'd had / had had more time.
  6. I wish I hadn't moved (to my new flat). (or: ...wish I'd stayed where I was)
