Unit 133 • Prepositions

Verb + preposition 2 about/for/of/afterVerbs with about; verbs with for; care about vs care for vs take care of; look for vs look after

Explore verbs taking 'about' (talk about; but discuss with no prep), 'for' (apply for, search for), and key distinctions among 'care' and 'look' phrases.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master verbs taking about: talk about, know about, do something/nothing about.
  • Avoid the preposition with discuss (discuss the problem, not *discuss about*).
  • Use for with search and application: apply for, search for, look for, leave for.
  • Differentiate care for (look after / like), care about (consider important), and take care of.
  • Distinguish between look for (seek) and look after (take care of).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Verb + About

We use about with verbs of speech, knowledge, and action:

talk / read / know ABOUT... • We talked about a lot of things.
do something / nothing ABOUT something (= take action to solve/improve):
If you’re worried about the problem, you should do something about it.
have a discussion ABOUT... • We had a discussion about the plan.

Warning: We say discuss something without any preposition:

We discussed the problem. (not discussed about the problem)

Part B: Verb + For

We use for when seeking, applying, or waiting:

apply (to a company) FOR a job • Why don’t you apply for it?
search (a place / person) FOR something • I searched the house for my keys.
wait FOR somebody / something • I’m waiting for the rain to stop.
leave (a place) FOR another place • She left home for work. (not left to work)
ask (somebody) FOR something • ask for a receipt
(Notice: ask somebody the way / the time — without 'for')

Part C: Care About vs Care For vs Take Care Of

Care About

care about = think that something is important:

He’s selfish. He doesn’t care about other people.

(Notice: I don’t care what / where / how — no 'about')

Care For & Take Care Of

care for somebody = look after, keep safe: He needs somebody to care for him.

care for something = like it (negative): I don’t care for hot weather.

take care of = look after, handle: I’ll take care of the tickets.

Part D: Look For vs Look After

Look For (= Seek)

I’ve lost my keys. Can you help me to look for them?

She is looking for a new job.

Look After (= Take Care Of)

He is elderly and needs someone to look after him. (not look for)

Please look after my dog while I’m away.
