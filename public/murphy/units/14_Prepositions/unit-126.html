Unit 126 • Prepositions

to, at, in and intomovement (to/into) vs position (at/in); arrive in/at; get into/out of

Contrast movement towards a destination (to, into) with position or arrival (at, in), and learn rules for home (no preposition) and vehicle actions (get in/out of vs get on/off).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use to for movement towards a destination or event: go to China, travel to Paris, welcome to England, been to Italy.
  • Distinguish movement (to) from position (in / at): They are going to France vs They live in France.
  • Use arrive in (town/country) and arrive at (building/event); never say arrive to.
  • Omit prepositions before home: go home, get home, arrive home, on my way home.
  • Use into (enter) vs out of; use get on / get off for buses, trains, and planes.

Part A — Theory

Part A: to for Movement vs in/at for Position

to • Movement to a place/event

go / come / travel / fly / walk / return to
• When are you going back to Italy?
• Three people were taken to hospital.
• Welcome to our country! (not welcome in)
• I've been to Italy four times.

in / at • Position / Being there

live / stay / be in or at
• They are going to France. → They live in France.
• Can you come to the party? → I'll see you at the party.

Part B: get and arrive

• get to a place: They got to the hotel at midnight. • What time did you get to work?
• arrive in a town or country: They arrived in Madrid / in Spain a week ago.
• arrive at buildings or events: What time did you arrive at the airport / at the party?
• Never say 'arrive to': not arrived to Spain / to the hotel.

Part C: home • No Preposition

We say go home, come home, get home, arrive home, on the way home, walk home (no preposition):
• What time did you get home? (not get to home)
• I met Lisa on my way home.

Part D: into vs out of • get on / off

into / out of • Rooms & Cars

go into, get into, walk into = enter:
• I opened the door and went into the room.
• She got into the car and drove away.
• Opposite: out of (got out of the car).

get on / off • Public Transport

For buses, trains, planes, and bikes:
• She got on the bus.
• You need to get off at the next station.
