Unit 130 • Prepositions

Adjective + preposition 1nice of you to …; kind to; angry with/about; excited about

Master high-frequency adjective complements: evaluating actions with it was kind/nice of you to do; behavior towards people with kind/polite to; emotional reactions with angry with somebody about something; and astonishment with amazed/shocked at/by.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use it is/was + adjective + of somebody (to do something): kind of, nice of, polite of, stupid of, generous of, careless of.
  • Use adjective + to somebody for interpersonal conduct: kind to, polite to, rude to, mean to, unpleasant to.
  • Use angry / annoyed / furious / upset WITH somebody FOR doing something vs ABOUT something.
  • Use excited / worried / nervous / happy ABOUT a situation.
  • Use surprised / shocked / amazed / astonished AT or BY something.
  • Use impressed WITH or BY • fed up / bored WITH.

Part A — Theory

Part A: It is/was + adjective + OF somebody

We say it is / was + adjective + of somebody (to do something):
nice of • kind of • good of • generous of • polite of • silly of • stupid of • careless of
• Thank you. That was very kind of you.
• It was stupid of me to forget my keys.

Part B: adjective + TO somebody (Behavior)

We say adjective + to somebody to describe behavior towards someone:
nice to • kind to • good to • polite to • rude to • mean to • unpleasant to
• They have always been very nice to me. (not nice with me)
• Why were you so rude to Mark?

Part C: Feelings • with, about, at, by

angry / annoyed / furious

• with somebody for doing:
Are you angry with me for being late?
• about something:
What are you so angry about?

excited / worried / upset

• excited / worried / nervous about:
Are you excited about going away?
• upset / happy about / with:
She's upset about not getting the job.

surprised / shocked / amazed

• at or by something:
Everybody was surprised at / by the news.
I was amazed at / by her performance.

impressed / bored / fed up

• impressed with or by:
I'm very impressed by / with her English.
• fed up with / bored with:
I'm bored with doing the same thing.
