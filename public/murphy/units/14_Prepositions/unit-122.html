Unit 122 • Prepositions

on time and in time · at the end and in the endpunctual vs soon enough; at the time when something ends vs finally

Master the critical distinctions between on time (punctual, at the planned time) and in time (soon enough, before it's too late), and between at the end (the point when something finishes) and in the end (finally, the ultimate outcome).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Contrast on time (punctual, according to schedule) and in time (soon enough for something / to do something).
  • Use just in time for situations where something happened almost too late.
  • Differentiate at the end of (something) (the closing point of an event or period) from in the end (finally / eventually).
  • Identify the opposites: too late is opposite in time; at the beginning is opposite at the end; at first is opposite in the end.

Part A — Theory

Part A: on time vs in time

on time = punctual, not late

If something happens on time, it happens at the scheduled or planned time:
• The 11.45 train left on time. (= it left at 11.45)
• Please be on time. Don't be late.
• The conference was well-organised. Everything began and finished on time.

in time = soon enough

in time (for something / to do something) means soon enough, not too late:
• Will you be home in time for dinner?
• I want to get home in time to watch the game.
• Opposite: too late (I got home too late to watch the game).
• just in time (= almost too late): We managed to stop just in time.

Part B: at the end vs in the end

at the end (of something)

At the time when something ends (followed by of):
• at the end of the month / January
• at the end of the film / game / concert
• The players shook hands at the end of the game.
• We do not say 'in the end of...'.
• Opposite: at the beginning (at the beginning of January).

in the end = finally

Used to say what the final result or outcome of a situation was:
• We had a lot of problems with our car. We sold it in the end. (= finally)
• Alan couldn't decide where to go. He didn't go anywhere in the end.
• Opposite: at first (At first we didn't get on, but in the end we became friends).
