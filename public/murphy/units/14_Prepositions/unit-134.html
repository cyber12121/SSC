Unit 134 • Prepositions

Verb + preposition 3 about and ofHear about vs of vs from; think about vs think of; dream, complain, and remind

Master subtle differences in meaning between 'hear about/of/from', 'think about/of', 'complain about/of', and 'remind about/of'.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Master the differences between hear about (be told), hear of (know exists), and hear from (receive communication).
  • Differentiate think about (consider/concentrate) and think of (invent, recall, give opinion).
  • Use dream about (while asleep) vs dream of/about (imagine/aspire).
  • Contrast complain about (dissatisfaction) with complain of (pain/illness).
  • Use remind about (not to forget) vs remind of (cause to remember / resemble).

Part A — Theory

Part A: Hear About vs Hear Of vs Hear From

Hear About & Hear Of

hear ABOUT = be told about something:
Did you hear about the fire at the hotel?

hear OF = know that somebody/something exists:
Who is Tom Hart? I’ve never heard of him.

Hear From

hear FROM = receive a message/call/letter from somebody:
Have you heard from Jane recently? • Yes, she called yesterday.

Part B: Think About vs Think Of

Think About (Consider / Concentrate)

think about = give active mental attention to:

I’ve thought about your advice.
Will you lend me the money? — I’ll think about it.
Think Of (Idea, Memory, Opinion)

think of = produce an idea, recall, or state an opinion:

It was my idea. I thought of it first.
I couldn’t think of anything to say.
What did you think of the film?

When considering possible future actions, both are possible: thinking of / thinking about buying a car.

Part C: Dream, Complain, and Remind

  • dream ABOUT (in sleep): I dreamt about you last night.
    dream OF / ABOUT (imagine): Do you dream of being rich? • I wouldn’t dream of doing that!
  • complain ABOUT (dissatisfaction): We complained about the bad food.
    complain OF (pain/symptoms): He was complaining of a pain in his back.
  • remind somebody ABOUT (tell not to forget): Please remind me about the meeting tomorrow.
    remind somebody OF (resemble / bring back memory): This house reminds me of my grandmother's home.
