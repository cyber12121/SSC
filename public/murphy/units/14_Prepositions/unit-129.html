Unit 129 • Prepositions

Noun + prepositionreason for, cause of, connection between, increase in etc.

Master noun + preposition patterns: noun + for (demand, reason), noun + of (cause, picture), noun + in (rise, drop), noun + to (solution, damage), and noun + with/between (relationship, link).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use noun + for: demand for, need for, reason for.
  • Use noun + of: cause of, photograph/picture of, advantage/disadvantage of.
  • Use noun + in: increase in, decrease in, rise in, fall in.
  • Use noun + to: damage to, invitation to, reaction to, solution to, attitude to/towards, answer/reply to, key to.
  • Use noun + with / between: relationship/connection/contact with; difference/connection between two things.

Part A — Theory

Part A: noun + for • noun + of

noun + for

a demand / a need for:
The company had to close because there wasn't enough demand for its products.
a reason for:
The train was late, but nobody knew the reason for the delay.

noun + of

a cause of:
The cause of the explosion is not known.
a picture / photo / photograph of:
Rachel showed me some photos of her family.
an advantage / a disadvantage of:
The advantage of living alone is that you can do what you like.

Part B: noun + in • noun + to

noun + in

an increase / a decrease / a rise / a fall in:
There has been an increase in the number of road accidents recently.
a rise in food prices / a drop in sales

noun + to

damage to: The accident was my fault, so I had to pay for the damage to the other car.
an invitation to (a party / a wedding)
a solution to (a problem) • a key to (a door)
an answer to • a reply to
an attitude to / towards

Part C: noun + with • noun + between

• a relationship / a connection / contact with:
Do you have a good relationship with your parents? • The police want to question a man in connection with the robbery.
• a relationship / a connection / contact / a difference between two things:
The police believe there is a connection between the two crimes. • What's the difference between a violin and a cello?
