Unit 131 • Prepositions

Adjective + preposition 2proud of; good at; married to; different from/to; full of

Continue mastering adjective complements: adjective + of (afraid of, aware of, proud of, capable of, short of, typical of), adjective + at (good/bad/hopeless at), adjective + to (similar to, married to), and adjective + with/from (crowded with, different from/to).

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  • Use adjective + of: afraid of, frightened of, proud of, ashamed of, jealous of, aware of, capable of, short of, full of, typical of.
  • Use good / bad / hopeless / brilliant AT an activity or subject.
  • Use married / engaged TO somebody (never with).
  • Use different FROM or different TO (American also: different than).
  • Use similar TO • crowded WITH • famous FOR • keen ON • dependent ON.

Part A — Theory

Part A: adjective + OF

• afraid / scared / frightened of: Are you afraid of spiders?
• proud / ashamed of: I'm proud of my children.
• jealous / envious of: Why are you so jealous of other people?
• aware / conscious of: Were you aware of the problem?
• capable / incapable of: He is capable of doing much better work.
• short of (= not having enough): I'm a bit short of money.
• typical of: He arrived late. That's typical of him.
• tired of / sick of: I'm tired of waiting.
• full of: The room was full of people.

Part B: adjective + AT

We use at with skills, abilities, and performance:
good at • bad at • brilliant at • hopeless at • excellent at
• I'm not very good at maths.
• Helen does her job very well. She's good at her job.
• I'm hopeless at telling jokes.

Part C: adjective + TO / FROM / WITH / ON / FOR

married to • similar to

• married to / engaged to (never with):
Steven is married to a doctor.
(not married with a doctor)
• similar to:
Your camera is similar to mine.

different from / to

• different from OR different to:
My problem is different from yours.
(American English also uses different than)

• interested in: I'm not interested in sport.
• keen on: We're not very keen on the idea.
• dependent on: He is completely dependent on his parents.
• crowded with: The platform was crowded with tourists.
• famous for: The city is famous for its cathedral.
• responsible for: Who is responsible for this mess?
